00059 | INDICATIONS AND USAGE
00060 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are an opioid agonist indicated in adults for the management of pain
00061 | severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments are inadequate (1).
00062 | Limitations of Use
00063 | Because of the risks of addiction, abuse, misuse, overdose, and death, which can occur at any dosage or
00064 | duration and persist over the course of therapy, reserve opioid analgesics, including tramadol
00065 | hydrochloride tablets for use in patients for whom alternative treatment options are ineffective, not
00066 | tolerated, or would be otherwise inadequate to provide sufficient management of pain. (1, 5.1)

00072 | doses are insufficiently effective and in whom the expected benefits of using a higher dose opioid

00117 | WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
00118 | Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia and Allodynia: Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) occurs when an opioid
00119 | analgesic paradoxically causes an increase in pain, or an increase in sensitivity to pain. If OIH is
00120 | suspected, carefully consider appropriately decreasing the dose of the current opioid analgesic, or
00121 | opioid rotation. (5.8)
00122 | Serotonin Syndrome: May be life-threatening. Can occur with use of tramadol alone, with concomitant
00123 | use of serotonergic drugs, with drugs that impair metabolism of serotonin or tramadol (5.9).
00124 | Risk of Seizure: Can occur at the recommended dose of tramadol. Concomitant use with other drugs
00125 | may increase seizure risk. Risk may increase in patients with epilepsy, a history of seizures, and in
00126 | patients with a recognized risk for seizures (5.10).
00127 | Risk of Suicide: Do not prescribe for suicidal or addiction-prone patients (5.11).
00128 | Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression in Patients with Chronic Pulmonary Disease or in Elderly,
00129 | Cachectic, or Debilitated Patients: Regularly evaluate closely, particularly during initiation and titration
00130 | (5.12).
00131 | Adrenal Insufficiency: If diagnosed, treat with physiologic replacement of corticosteroids, and wean
00132 | patient off the opioid (5.13).
00133 | Severe Hypotension: Regularly evaluate during dosage initiation and titration. Avoid use of tramadol
00134 | hydrochloride tablets in patients with circulatory shock (5.14).
00135 | Risks of Use in Patients with Increased Intracranial Pressure, Brain Tumors, Head Injury, or Impaired
00136 | Consciousness: Regularly evaluate for sedation and respiratory depression. Avoid use of tramadol
00137 | hydrochloride tablets in patients with impaired consciousness or coma (5.15).

00340 | Initiating Treatment with Tramadol Hydrochloride Tablets
00341 | Initiate treatment at the lowest dose necessary to achieve adequate analgesia. Titrate
00342 | the dose based upon the individual patient's response to their initial dose of tramadol
00343 | hydrochloride tablets.
00344 | For patients not requiring rapid onset of analgesic effect, the tolerability of tramadol
00345 | hydrochloride tablets can be improved by initiating therapy with the following titration
00346 | regimen: Start tramadol hydrochloride tablets at 25 mg/day and titrated in 25 mg
00347 | increments as separate doses every 3 days to reach 100 mg/day (25 mg four times a
00348 | day). Thereafter the total daily dose may be increased by 50 mg as tolerated every 3
00349 | days to reach 200 mg/day (50 mg four times a day). After titration, tramadol
00350 | hydrochloride tablets 50 to 100 mg can be administered as needed for pain relief every
00351 | 4 to 6 hours not to exceed 400 mg/day.
00352 | 
00353 | For the subset of patients for whom rapid onset of analgesic effect is required and for
00354 | whom the benefits outweigh the risk of discontinuation due to adverse events
00355 | associated with higher initial doses, tramadol hydrochloride tablets 50 mg to 100 mg can
00356 | be administered as needed for pain relief every four to six hours, not to exceed 400 mg
00357 | per day.
00358 | Conversion from Tramadol hydrochloride tablets to Extended-Release Tramadol
00359 | The relative bioavailability of tramadol hydrochloride tablets compared to extended-
00360 | release tramadol is unknown, so conversion to extended-release formulations may lead
00361 | to increased risk of excessive sedation and respiratory depression.
00362 | Dosage Modification in Patients with Hepatic Impairment
00363 | The recommended dose for adult patients with severe hepatic impairment is 50 mg
00364 | every 12 hours.
00365 | Dosage Modification in Patients with Renal Impairment
00366 | In all patients with creatinine clearance less than 30 mL/min, it is recommended that the
00367 | dosing interval of tramadol hydrochloride tablets be increased to 12 hours, with a
00368 | maximum daily dose of 200 mg. Since only 7% of an administered dose is removed by
00369 | hemodialysis, dialysis patients can receive their regular dose on the day of dialysis.
00370 | Dosage Modification in Geriatric Patients
00371 | Do not exceed a total dose of 300 mg/day in patients over 75 years old.
00372 | 2.4 Titration and Maintenance of Therapy
00373 | Individually titrate tramadol hydrochloride tablets to a dose that provides adequate
00374 | analgesia and minimizes adverse reactions. Continually reevaluate patients receiving
00375 | tramadol hydrochloride tablets to assess the maintenance of pain control, signs and
00376 | symptoms of opioid withdrawal, and other adverse reactions, as well as to reassess for
00377 | the development of addiction, abuse, or misuse [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1),
00378 | (5.18)]. Frequent communication is important among the prescriber, other members of
00379 | the healthcare team, the patient, and the caregiver/family during periods of changing
00380 | analgesic requirements, including initial titration.
00381 | If the level of pain increases after dosage stabilization, attempt to identify the source of
00382 | increased pain before increasing the tramadol hydrochloride tablets dosage. If after
00383 | increasing the dosage, unacceptable opioid-related adverse reactions are observed
00384 | (including an increase in pain after dosage increase), consider reducing the dosage [see
00385 | Warnings and Precautions (5)]. Adjust the dosage to obtain an appropriate balance
00386 | between management of pain and opioid-related adverse reactions.
00387 | 2.5 Safe Reduction and Discontinuation of Tramadol Hydrochloride Tablets
00388 | Do not rapidly reduce or abruptly discontinue tramadol hydrochloride tablets in patients
00389 | who may be physically dependent on opioids. Rapid reduction or abrupt discontinuation
00390 | of opioid analgesics in patients who are physically dependent on opioids has resulted in
00391 | serious withdrawal symptoms, uncontrolled pain, and suicide. Rapid reduction or abrupt
00392 | discontinuation has also been associated with attempts to find other sources of opioid
00393 | analgesics, which may be confused with drug-seeking for abuse. Patients may also
00394 | attempt to treat their pain or withdrawal symptoms with illicit opioids, such as heroin,
00395 | 
00396 | and other substances.
00397 | When a decision has been made to decrease the dose or discontinue therapy in an
00398 | opioid-dependent patient taking tramadol hydrochloride tablets, there are a variety of
00399 | factors that should be considered, including the total daily dose of opioid (including
00400 | tramadol hydrochloride tablets) the patient has been taking, the duration of treatment,
00401 | the type of pain being treated, and the physical and psychological attributes of the
00402 | patient. It is important to ensure ongoing care of the patient and to agree on an
00403 | appropriate tapering schedule and follow-up plan so that patient and provider goals and
00404 | expectations are clear and realistic. When opioid analgesics are being discontinued due
00405 | to a suspected substance use disorder, evaluate and treat the patient, or refer for
00406 | evaluation and treatment of the substance use disorder. Treatment should include
00407 | evidence-based approaches, such as medication assisted treatment of opioid use
00408 | disorder. Complex patients with comorbid pain and substance use disorders may benefit
00409 | from referral to a specialist.
00410 | There are no standard opioid tapering schedules that are suitable for all patients. Good
00411 | clinical practice dictates a patient-specific plan to taper the dose of the opioid gradually.
00412 | For patients on tramadol hydrochloride tablets who are physically opioid-dependent,
00413 | initiate the taper by a small enough increment, (e.g., no greater than 10% to 25% of the
00414 | total daily dose) to avoid withdrawal symptoms, and proceed with dose-lowering at an
00415 | interval of every 2 to 4 weeks. Patients who have been taking opioids for briefer periods
00416 | of time may tolerate a more rapid taper.
00417 | It may be necessary to provide the patient with a lower dosage strength to accomplish a
00418 | successful taper. Reassess the patient frequently to manage pain and withdrawal
00419 | symptoms, should they emerge. Common withdrawal symptoms include restlessness,
00420 | lacrimation, rhinorrhea, yawning, perspiration, chills, myalgia, and mydriasis. Other signs
00421 | and symptoms also may develop, including irritability, anxiety, backache, joint pain,
00422 | weakness, abdominal cramps, insomnia, nausea, anorexia, vomiting, diarrhea, or
00423 | increased blood pressure, respiratory rate, or heart rate. If withdrawal symptoms arise,
00424 | it may be necessary to pause the taper for a period of time or raise the dose of the
00425 | opioid analgesic to the previous dose, and then proceed with a slower taper. In addition,
00426 | regularly evaluate patients for any changes in mood, emergence of suicidal thoughts, or
00427 | use of other substances.
00428 | When managing patients taking opioid analgesics, particularly those who have been
00429 | treated for an extended period of time, and/or with high doses for chronic pain, ensure
00430 | that a multimodal approach to pain management, including mental health support (if
00431 | needed), is in place prior to initiating an opioid analgesic taper. A multimodal approach to
00432 | pain management may optimize the treatment of chronic pain, as well as assist with the
00433 | successful tapering of the opioid analgesic [see Warnings and Precautions (5.18), Drug

00460 | 5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
00461 | 5.1 Addiction, Abuse and Misuse
00462 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets contains tramadol, a Schedule IV controlled substance.
00463 | As an opioid, tramadol hydrochloride tablets exposes users to the risks of addiction,
00464 | abuse, and misuse [see Drug Abuse and Dependence (9)].
00465 | Although the risk of addiction in any individual is unknown, it can occur in patients
00466 | appropriately prescribed tramadol hydrochloride tablets. Addiction can occur at
00467 | recommended dosages and if the drug is misused or abused. The risk of opioid-related
00468 | overdose or overdose-related death is increased with higher opioid doses, and this risk
00469 | persists over the course of therapy. In postmarketing studies, addiction, abuse, misuse,
00470 | and fatal and non-fatal opioid overdose were observed in patients with long-term opioid
00471 | use [see Adverse Reactions (6)].
00472 | Assess each patient's risk for opioid addiction, abuse, or misuse prior to prescribing
00473 | tramadol hydrochloride tablets, and reassess all patients receiving tramadol
00474 | hydrochloride tablets for the development of these behaviors and conditions. Risks are
00475 | increased in patients with a personal or family history of substance abuse (including
00476 | drug or alcohol abuse or addiction) or mental illness (e.g., major depression). The
00477 | potential for these risks should not, however, prevent the proper management of pain in
00478 | any given patient. Patients at increased risk may be prescribed opioids such as tramadol
00479 | hydrochloride tablets, but use in such patients necessitates intensive counseling about
00480 | the risks and proper use of tramadol hydrochloride tablets along with frequent
00481 | reevaluation for signs of addiction, abuse, and misuse. Consider recommending or
00482 | prescribing an opioid overdose reversal agent [see Dosage and Administration (2.2),
00483 | Warnings and Precautions (5.2)].
00484 | 
00485 | Opioids are sought for nonmedical use and are subject to diversion from legitimate
00486 | prescribed use. Consider these risks when prescribing or dispensing tramadol
00487 | hydrochloride tablets. Strategies to reduce these risks include prescribing the drug in
00488 | the smallest appropriate quantity and advising the patient on careful storage of the drug
00489 | during the course of treatment and proper disposal of unused drug. Contact local state
00490 | professional licensing board or state-controlled substances authority for information on
00491 | how to prevent and detect abuse or diversion of this product.
00492 | 5.2 Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression
00493 | Serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression has been reported with the use
00494 | of opioids, even when used as recommended. Respiratory depression, if not immediately
00495 | recognized and treated, may lead to respiratory arrest and death. Management of
00496 | respiratory depression may include close observation, supportive measures, and use of
00497 | opioid overdose reversal agents, depending on the patient's clinical status [see
00498 | Overdosage (10)]. Carbon dioxide (CO ) retention from opioid-induced respiratory
00499 | depression can exacerbate the sedating effects of opioids.
00500 | While serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression can occur at any time
00501 | during the use of tramadol hydrochloride tablets, the risk is greatest during the initiation
00502 | of therapy or following a dosage increase.
00503 | To reduce the risk of respiratory depression, proper dosing and titration of tramadol
00504 | hydrochloride tablets are essential [see Dosage and Administration (2)]. Overestimating
00505 | the tramadol hydrochloride tablets dosage when converting patients from another
00506 | opioid product can result in a fatal overdose with the first dose.
00507 | Accidental ingestion of even one dose of tramadol hydrochloride tablets, especially by
00508 | children, can result in respiratory depression and death due to an overdose of tramadol.
00509 | Educate patients and caregivers on how to recognize respiratory depression and
00510 | emphasize the importance of calling 911 or getting emergency medical help right away in
00511 | the event of a known or suspected overdose [see Patient Counseling Information (17)].
00512 | Opioids can cause sleep-related breathing disorders including central sleep apnea (CSA)
00513 | and sleep-related hypoxemia. Opioid use increases the risk of CSA in a dose-dependent
00514 | fashion. In patients who present with CSA, consider decreasing the opioid dosage using
00515 | best practices for opioid taper [see Dosage and Administration (2.5)].
00516 | Patient Access to an Opioid Overdose Reversal Agent for the Emergency Treatment of
00517 | Opioid Overdose
00518 | Inform patients and caregivers about opioid overdose reversal agents (e.g., naloxone,
00519 | nalmefene). Discuss the importance of having access to an opioid overdose reversal
00520 | agent, especially if the patient has risk factors for overdose (e.g., concomitant use of
00521 | CNS depressants, a history of opioid use disorder, or prior opioid overdose) or if there
00522 | are household members (including children) or other close contacts at risk for accidental
00523 | ingestion or opioid overdose. The presence of risk factors for overdose should not
00524 | prevent the management of pain in any patient [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1,
00525 | 5.3)].
00526 | Discuss the options for obtaining an opioid overdose reversal agent (e.g., prescription,
00527 | over-the-counter, or as part of a community-based program).
00528 | 2
00529 | 
00530 | There are important differences among the opioid overdose reversal agents, such as
00531 | route of administration, product strength, approved patient age range, and
00532 | pharmacokinetics. Be familiar with these differences, as outlined in the approved labeling
00533 | for those products, prior to recommending or prescribing such an agent.
00534 | Educate patients and caregivers on how to recognize respiratory depression, and how

00539 | 5.3 Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS
00540 | Depressants
00541 | Profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death may result from the
00542 | concomitant use of tramadol hydrochloride tablets with benzodiazepines and/or other
00543 | CNS depressants, including alcohol (e.g., non-benzodiazepine sedatives/hypnotics,
00544 | anxiolytics, tranquilizers, muscle relaxants, general anesthetics, antipsychotics,
00545 | gabapentinoids [gabapentin or pregabalin], and other opioids). Because of these risks,
00546 | reserve concomitant prescribing of these drugs for use in patients for whom alternative
00547 | treatment options are inadequate.

00549 | benzodiazepines increases the risk of drug-related mortality compared to use of opioid
00550 | analgesics alone. Because of similar pharmacological properties, it is reasonable to
00551 | expect similar risk with the concomitant use of other CNS depressant drugs with opioid
00552 | analgesics [see Drug Interactions (7)].

00554 | concomitantly with an opioid analgesic, prescribe the lowest effective dosages and
00555 | minimum durations of concomitant use. In patients already receiving an opioid analgesic,
00556 | prescribe a lower initial dose of the benzodiazepine or other CNS depressant than
00557 | indicated in the absence of an opioid, and titrate based on clinical response. If an opioid
00558 | analgesic is initiated in a patient already taking a benzodiazepine or other CNS
00559 | depressant, prescribe a lower initial dose of the opioid analgesic, and titrate based on
00560 | clinical response. Inform patients and caregivers of this potential interaction and educate
00561 | them on the signs and symptoms of respiratory depression (including sedation).
00562 | If concomitant use is warranted, consider recommending or prescribing an opioid
00563 | overdose reversal agent [see Dosage and Administration (2.2), Warnings and
00564 | Precautions (5.2) and Overdosage (10)].
00565 | Advise both patients and caregivers about the risks of respiratory depression and
00566 | sedation when tramadol hydrochloride tablets are used with benzodiazepines or other
00567 | CNS depressants (including alcohol and illicit drugs). Advise patients not to drive or
00568 | operate heavy machinery until the effects of concomitant use of the benzodiazepine or
00569 | other CNS depressant have been determined. Screen patients for risk of substance use
00570 | disorders, including opioid abuse and misuse, and warn them of the risk for overdose
00571 | and death associated with the use of additional CNS depressants including alcohol and
00572 | illicit drugs [see Drug Interactions (7); and Patient Counseling Information (17)].

00609 | threatening Respiratory Depression in Children
00610 | Life-threatening respiratory depression and death have occurred in children who
00611 | received tramadol. Tramadol and codeine are subject to variability in metabolism based
00612 | upon CYP2D6 genotype (described below), which can lead to increased exposure to an
00613 | active metabolite. Based upon postmarketing reports with tramadol or with codeine,

00616 | are treated with opioids for post-tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy pain may be
00617 | particularly sensitive to their respiratory depressant effect. Because of the risk of life-
00618 | threatening respiratory depression and death:
00619 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are contraindicated for all children younger than 12
00620 | years of age [see Contraindications (4)].

00622 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are contraindicated for postoperative management in

00624 | adenoidectomy [see Contraindications (4)].
00625 | Avoid the use of tramadol hydrochloride tablets in adolescents 12 to 18 years of age
00626 | who have other risk factors that may increase their sensitivity to the respiratory

00631 | As with adults, when prescribing opioids for adolescents, healthcare providers should

00633 | and caregivers about these risks and the signs of opioid overdose [see Use in

00635 | Nursing Mothers
00636 | Tramadol is subject to the same polymorphic metabolism as codeine, with ultra-rapid
00637 | metabolizers of CYP2D6 substrates being potentially exposed to life-threatening levels of

00645 | CYP2D6 Genetic Variability: Ultra-rapid Metabolizer
00646 | Some individuals may be ultra-rapid metabolizers because of a specific CYP2D6
00647 | genotype (e.g., gene duplications denoted as *1/*1xN or *1/*2xN). The prevalence of
00648 | this CYP2D6 phenotype varies widely and has been estimated at 1 to 10% for Whites
00649 | (European, North American), 3 to 4% for Blacks (African Americans), 1 to 2% for East
00650 | Asians (Chinese, Japanese, Korean), and may be greater than 10% in certain racial/ethnic
00651 | groups (i.e., Oceanian, Northern African, Middle Eastern, Ashkenazi Jews, Puerto Rican).
00652 | These individuals convert tramadol into its active metabolite, O-desmethyltramadol (M1),
00653 | more rapidly and completely than other people. This rapid conversion results in higher
00654 | than expected serum M1 levels. Even at labeled dosage regimens, individuals who are
00655 | ultra-rapid metabolizers may have life-threatening or fatal respiratory depression or
00656 | experience signs of overdose (such as extreme sleepiness, confusion, or shallow
00657 | breathing) [see Overdosage (10)]. Therefore, individuals who are ultra-rapid metabolizers
00658 | should not use tramadol hydrochloride tablets.

00703 | 5.8 Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia and Allodynia
00704 | Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) occurs when an opioid analgesic paradoxically
00705 | causes an increase in pain, or an increase in sensitivity to pain. This condition differs
00706 | from tolerance, which is the need for increasing doses of opioids to maintain a defined
00707 | effect [see Dependence (9.3)]. Symptoms of OIH include (but may not be limited to)
00708 | increased levels of pain upon opioid dosage increase, decreased levels of pain upon
00709 | opioid dosage decrease, or pain from ordinarily non-painful stimuli (allodynia). These
00710 | symptoms may suggest OIH only if there is no evidence of underlying disease
00711 | progression, opioid tolerance, opioid withdrawal, or addictive behavior.
00712 | Cases of OIH have been reported, both with short-term and longer-term use of opioid
00713 | 
00714 | analgesics. Though the mechanism of OIH is not fully understood, multiple biochemical
00715 | pathways have been implicated. Medical literature suggests a strong biologic plausibility
00716 | between opioid analgesics and OIH and allodynia. If a patient is suspected to be
00717 | experiencing OIH, carefully consider appropriately decreasing the dose of the current
00718 | opioid analgesic or opioid rotation (safely switching the patient to a different opioid
00719 | moiety) [see Dosage and Administration (2.5); Warnings and Precautions (5.18)].
00720 | 5.9 Serotonin Syndrome Risk
00721 | Cases of serotonin syndrome, a potentially life-threatening condition, have been
00722 | reported with the use of tramadol, particularly during concomitant use with serotonergic
00723 | drugs. Serotonergic drugs include selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs),
00724 | serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs), tricyclic antidepressants
00725 | (TCAs), triptans, 5-HT3 receptor antagonists, drugs that affect the serotonergic
00726 | neurotransmitter system (e.g., mirtazapine, trazodone, tramadol), certain muscle
00727 | relaxants (i.e., cyclobenzaprine, metaxalone), and drugs that impair metabolism of
00728 | serotonin (including MAO inhibitors, both those intended to treat psychiatric disorders
00729 | and also others, such as linezolid and intravenous methylene blue) [see Drug
00730 | Interactions (7)]. This may occur within the recommended dosage range.
00731 | Serotonin syndrome symptoms may include mental status changes (e.g., agitation,
00732 | hallucinations, coma), autonomic instability (e.g., tachycardia, labile blood pressure,
00733 | hyperthermia), neuromuscular aberrations (e.g., hyperreflexia, incoordination, rigidity),
00734 | and/or gastrointestinal symptoms (e.g., nausea, vomiting, diarrhea). The onset of
00735 | symptoms generally occurs within several hours to a few days of concomitant use, but
00736 | may occur later than that. Discontinue tramadol hydrochloride tablets if serotonin
00737 | syndrome is suspected.

00788 | 5.13 Adrenal Insufficiency
00789 | Cases of adrenal insufficiency have been reported with opioid use, more often following

00796 | treatment until adrenal function recovers. Other opioids may be tried as some cases
00797 | reported use of a different opioid without recurrence of adrenal insufficiency. The
00798 | information available does not identify any particular opioids as being more likely to be
00799 | associated with adrenal insufficiency.

00865 | 5.20 Hyponatremia
00866 | Hyponatremia (serum sodium < 135 mmol/L) has been reported with the use of
00867 | tramadol, and many cases are severe (sodium level < 120 mmol/L). Most cases of
00868 | hyponatremia occurred in females over the age of 65 and within the first week of
00869 | therapy. In some reports, hyponatremia resulted from the syndrome of inappropriate
00870 | antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH). Regularly evaluate patients for signs and
00871 | symptoms of hyponatremia (e.g., confusion, disorientation), during treatment with
00872 | tramadol hydrochloride tablets, especially during initiation of therapy. If signs and
00873 | symptoms of hyponatremia are present, initiate appropriate treatment (e.g., fluid
00874 | restriction) and discontinue tramadol hydrochloride tablets [see Dosage and
00875 | Administration: Safe Reduction and Discontinuation (2.5)].

00877 | Cases of tramadol-associated hypoglycemia have been reported, some resulting in
00878 | hospitalization. In most cases, patients had predisposing risk factors (e.g. diabetes). If
00879 | hypoglycemia is suspected, monitor blood glucose levels and consider drug
00880 | discontinuation as appropriate [see Dosage and Administration: Safe Reduction and
00881 | Discontinuation (2.5)].

01303 | 8.1 Pregnancy
01304 | Risk Summary
01305 | Use of opioid analgesics for an extended period of time during pregnancy may cause
01306 | neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome [see Warnings and Precautions (5.8)].
01307 | Available data with tramadol hydrochloride tablets in pregnant women are insufficient to
01308 | inform a drug-associated risk for major birth defects and miscarriage.
01309 | In animal reproduction studies, tramadol administration during organogenesis decreased
01310 | fetal weights and reduced ossification in mice, rats, and rabbits at 1.4, 0.6, and 3.6
01311 | times the maximum recommended human daily dosage (MRHD). Tramadol decreased
01312 | pup body weight and increased pup mortality at 1.2 and 1.9 times the MRHD [see Data].
01313 | Based on animal data, advise pregnant women of the potential risk to a fetus.
01314 | The estimated background risk of major birth defects and miscarriage for the indicated
01315 | population is unknown. All pregnancies have a background risk of birth defect, loss, or
01316 | other adverse outcomes. In the U.S. general population, the estimated background risk
01317 | of major birth defects and miscarriage in clinically recognized pregnancies is 2 to 4% and
01318 | 15 to 20%, respectively.
01319 | Clinical Considerations
01320 | Fetal/Neonatal Adverse Reactions
01321 | Use of opioid analgesics for an extended period of time during pregnancy for medical or
01322 | nonmedical purposes can result in physical dependence in the neonate and neonatal
01323 | opioid withdrawal syndrome shortly after birth.
01324 | Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome can present as irritability, hyperactivity and
01325 | abnormal sleep pattern, high pitched cry, tremor, vomiting, diarrhea and failure to gain
01326 | weight. The onset, duration, and severity of neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome vary
01327 | based on the specific opioid used, duration of use, timing and amount of last maternal
01328 | use, and rate of elimination of the drug by the newborn. Observe newborns for
01329 | symptoms and signs of neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome and manage accordingly
01330 | [see Warnings and Precautions (5.5)].
01331 | 
01332 | Neonatal seizures, neonatal withdrawal syndrome, fetal death and still birth have been
01333 | reported during postmarketing.
01334 | Labor or Delivery
01335 | Opioids cross the placenta and may produce respiratory depression and psycho-
01336 | physiologic effects in neonates. An opioid overdose reversal agent, such as naloxone or
01337 | nalmefene, must be available for reversal of opioid-induced respiratory depression in the
01338 | neonate. Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are not recommended for use in pregnant
01339 | women during or immediately prior to labor, when other analgesic techniques are more
01340 | appropriate. Opioid analgesics, including tramadol hydrochloride tablets, can prolong
01341 | labor through actions which temporarily reduce the strength, duration, and frequency of
01342 | uterine contractions. However, this effect is not consistent and may be offset by an
01343 | increased rate of cervical dilation, which tends to shorten labor. Monitor neonates
01344 | exposed to opioid analgesics during labor for signs of excess sedation and respiratory
01345 | depression.
01346 | Tramadol has been shown to cross the placenta. The mean ratio of serum tramadol in
01347 | the umbilical veins compared to maternal veins was 0.83 for 40 women given tramadol
01348 | during labor.
01349 | The effect of tramadol hydrochloride tablets, if any, on the later growth, development,
01350 | and functional maturation of the child is unknown.

01405 | 8.4 Pediatric Use
01406 | The safety and effectiveness of tramadol hydrochloride tablets in pediatric patients have
01407 | not been established.
01408 | Life-threatening respiratory depression and death have occurred in children who
01409 | received tramadol [see Warnings and Precautions (5.6)]. In some of the reported cases,
01410 | these events followed tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy, and one of the children had
01411 | evidence of being an ultra-rapid metabolizer of tramadol (i.e., multiple copies of the gene
01412 | for cytochrome P450 isoenzyme 2D6). Children with sleep apnea may be particularly
01413 | sensitive to the respiratory depressant effects of tramadol. Because of the risk of life-
01414 | threatening respiratory depression and death:
01415 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are contraindicated for all children younger than 12
01416 | years of age [see Contraindications (4)].
01417 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are contraindicated for postoperative management in
01418 | pediatric patients younger than 18 years of age following tonsillectomy and/or
01419 | adenoidectomy [see Contraindications (4)].
01420 | 
01421 | Avoid the use of tramadol hydrochloride tablets in adolescents 12 to 18 years of age
01422 | who have other risk factors that may increase their sensitivity to the respiratory

01424 | include conditions associated with hypoventilation such as postoperative status,
01425 | obstructive sleep apnea, obesity, severe pulmonary disease, neuromuscular disease,
01426 | and concomitant use of other medications that cause respiratory depression.
01427 | 8.5 Geriatric Use
01428 | A total of 455 elderly (65 years of age or older) subjects were exposed to tramadol
01429 | hydrochloride tablets in controlled clinical trials. Of those, 145 subjects were 75 years of
01430 | age and older.
01431 | In studies including geriatric patients, treatment-limiting adverse events were higher in
01432 | subjects over 75 years of age compared to those under 65 years of age. Specifically,
01433 | 30% of those over 75 years of age had gastrointestinal treatment-limiting adverse
01434 | events compared to 17% of those under 65 years of age. Constipation resulted in
01435 | discontinuation of treatment in 10% of those over 75.
01436 | Respiratory depression is the chief risk for elderly patients treated with opioids, and has
01437 | occurred after large initial doses were administered to patients who were not opioid-
01438 | tolerant or when opioids were co-administered with other agents that depress
01439 | respiration. Titrate the dosage of tramadol hydrochloride tablets slowly in geriatric
01440 | patients starting at the low end of the dosing range and frequently reevaluate the patient
01441 | for signs of central nervous system and respiratory depression [see Warnings and
01442 | Precautions (5.12)].
01443 | Tramadol is known to be substantially excreted by the kidney, and the risk of adverse
01444 | reactions to this drug may be greater in patients with impaired renal function. Because
01445 | elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, care should be taken in
01446 | dose selection, and it may be useful to monitor renal function.
01447 | 8.6 Renal and Hepatic Impairment
01448 | Impaired renal function results in a decreased rate and extent of excretion of tramadol
01449 | and its active metabolite, M1. In patients with creatinine clearances of less than 30
01450 | mL/min, dosing reduction is recommended [see Dosage and Administration (2.3)].
01451 | Metabolism of tramadol and M1 is reduced in patients with severe hepatic impairment
01452 | based on a study in patients with advanced cirrhosis of the liver. In patients with severe
01453 | hepatic impairment, dosing reduction is recommended [see Dosage and Administration
01454 | (2.3)].
01455 | With the prolonged half-life in these conditions, achievement of steady-state is delayed,
01456 | so that it may take several days for elevated plasma concentrations to develop.

01458 | 9.1 Controlled Substance
01459 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets contain tramadol, a Schedule IV controlled substance.
01460 | 9.2 Abuse
01461 | 
01462 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets contains tramadol, a substance with potential for misuse
01463 | and abuse, which can lead to the development of substance use disorder, including
01464 | addiction [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1)].
01465 | Misuse is the intentional use, for therapeutic purposes, of a drug by an individual in a
01466 | way other than prescribed by a healthcare provider or for whom it was not prescribed.
01467 | Abuse is the intentional, non-therapeutic use of a drug, even once, for its desirable
01468 | psychological or physiological effects.
01469 | Drug addiction is a cluster of behavioral, cognitive, and physiological phenomena that
01470 | may include a strong desire to take the drug, difficulties in controlling drug use (e.g.,
01471 | continuing drug use despite harmful consequences, giving a higher priority to drug use
01472 | than other activities and obligations), and possible tolerance or physical dependence.
01473 | Misuse and abuse of tramadol hydrochloride tablets increases risk of overdosage, which
01474 | may lead to central nervous system and respiratory depression, hypotension, seizures,
01475 | and death. The risk is increased with concurrent abuse of tramadol hydrochloride tablets
01476 | with alcohol and other CNS depressants. Abuse of and addiction to opioids in some
01477 | individuals may not be accompanied by concurrent tolerance and symptoms of physical
01478 | dependence. In addition, abuse of opioids can occur in the absence of addiction.
01479 | All patients treated with opioids require careful and frequent reevaluation for signs of
01480 | misuse, abuse, and addiction, because use of opioid analgesic products carries the risk
01481 | of addiction even under appropriate medical use. Patients at high risk of tramadol
01482 | hydrochloride tablets abuse include those with a history of prolonged use of any opioid,
01483 | including products containing tramadol, those with a history of drug or alcohol abuse, or
01484 | those who use tramadol hydrochloride tablets in combination with other abused drugs.
01485 | "Drug-seeking" behavior is very common in persons with substance use disorders.
01486 | Drug-seeking tactics include emergency calls or visits near the end of office hours,
01487 | refusal to undergo appropriate examination, testing, or referral, repeated "loss" of
01488 | prescriptions, tampering with prescriptions, and reluctance to provide prior medical
01489 | records or contact information for other treating healthcare provider(s). "Doctor
01490 | shopping" (visiting multiple prescribers to obtain additional prescriptions) is common
01491 | among people who abuse drugs and people with substance use disorder. Preoccupation
01492 | with achieving adequate pain relief can be appropriate behavior in a patient with
01493 | inadequate pain control.
01494 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets, like other opioids, can be diverted for nonmedical use
01495 | into illicit channels of distribution. Careful record-keeping of prescribing information,
01496 | including quantity, frequency, and renewal requests, as required by state and federal
01497 | law, is strongly advised.
01498 | Proper assessment of the patient, proper prescribing practices, periodic reevaluation of
01499 | therapy, and proper dispensing and storage are appropriate measures that help to limit
01500 | abuse of opioid drugs.
01501 | Risks Specific to Abuse of Tramadol hydrochloride tablets
01502 | Abuse of tramadol hydrochloride tablets poses a risk of overdose and death. The risk is
01503 | increased with concurrent abuse of tramadol hydrochloride tablets with alcohol and/or
01504 | other CNS depressants.
01505 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets are approved for oral use only. Parenteral drug abuse is
01506 | 
01507 | commonly associated with transmission of infectious diseases such as hepatitis and
01508 | HIV.
01509 | 9.3 Dependence
01510 | Both tolerance and physical dependence can develop during use of opioid therapy.
01511 | Tolerance is a physiological state characterized by a reduced response to a drug after
01512 | repeated administration (i.e., a higher dose of a drug is required to produce the same
01513 | effect that was once obtained at a lower dose).
01514 | Physical dependence is a state that develops as a result of a physiological adaptation in
01515 | response to repeated drug use, manifested by withdrawal signs and symptoms after
01516 | abrupt discontinuation or a significant dosage reduction of a drug.
01517 | Withdrawal may be precipitated through the administration of drugs with opioid
01518 | antagonist activity (e.g., naloxone, nalmefene), mixed agonist/antagonist analgesics
01519 | (e.g., pentazocine, butorphanol, nalbuphine), or partial agonists (e.g., buprenorphine).
01520 | Physical dependence may not occur to a clinically significant degree until after several
01521 | days to weeks of continued use.
01522 | Do not rapidly reduce or abruptly discontinue tramadol hydrochloride tablets in a patient
01523 | physically dependent on opioids. Rapid tapering of tramadol hydrochloride tablets in a
01524 | patient physically dependent on opioids may lead to serious withdrawal symptoms,
01525 | uncontrolled pain, and suicide. Rapid discontinuation has also been associated with
01526 | attempts to find other sources of opioid analgesics, which may be confused with drug-
01527 | seeking for abuse.
01528 | When discontinuing tramadol hydrochloride tablets, gradually taper the dosage using a
01529 | patient-specific plan that considers the following: the dose of tramadol hydrochloride
01530 | tablets the patient has been taking, the duration of treatment, and the physical and
01531 | psychological attributes of the patient. To improve the likelihood of a successful taper
01532 | and minimize withdrawal symptoms, it is important that the opioid tapering schedule is
01533 | agreed upon by the patient. In patients taking opioids for an extended period of time at
01534 | high doses, ensure that a multimodal approach to pain management, including mental
01535 | health support (if needed), is in place prior to initiating an opioid analgesic taper [see
01536 | Dosage and Administration (2.5), and Warnings and Precautions (5.18)].
01537 | Infants born to mothers physically dependent on opioids will also be physically
01538 | dependent and may exhibit respiratory difficulties and withdrawal signs [see Use in
01539 | Specific Populations (8.1)].
01540 | 10 OVERDOSAGE
01541 | Clinical Presentation
01542 | Acute overdosage with tramadol hydrochloride tablets can be manifested by respiratory
01543 | depression, somnolence progressing to stupor or coma, skeletal muscle flaccidity, cold
01544 | and clammy skin, constricted pupils, and, in some cases, pulmonary edema,
01545 | bradycardia, QT prolongation, hypotension, partial or complete airway obstruction,
01546 | atypical snoring, seizures, and death. Marked mydriasis rather than miosis may be seen
01547 | with hypoxia in overdose situations. Toxic leukoencephalopathy has been reported after
01548 | opioid overdose and can present hours, days, or weeks after apparent recovery from
01549 | 
01550 | the initial intoxication.
01551 | Deaths due to overdose have been reported with abuse and misuse of tramadol [see
01552 | Warnings and Precautions (5.1); Drug Abuse and Dependence (9.2)]. Review of case
01553 | reports has indicated that the risk of fatal overdose is further increased when tramadol
01554 | is abused concurrently with alcohol or other CNS depressants, including other opioids.
01555 | Treatment of Overdose
01556 | In case of overdose, priorities are the re-establishment of a patent and protected airway
01557 | and institution of assisted or controlled ventilation, if needed. Employ other supportive
01558 | measures (including oxygen and vasopressors) in the management of circulatory shock
01559 | and pulmonary edema as indicated. Cardiac arrest or serious arrhythmias will require
01560 | advanced life-supporting measures. Because strategies for the management of
01561 | overdose are continually evolving, it is advisable to contact a poison control center
01562 | (where available) to determine the latest recommendations for the management of an
01563 | overdose.
01564 | For clinically significant respiratory or circulatory depression secondary to opioid
01565 | overdose, administer an opioid overdose reversal agent such as naloxone or nalmefene.
01566 | While an opioid overdose reversal agent will reverse some, but not all, symptoms caused
01567 | by overdosage with tramadol, the risk of seizures is also increased with reversal agent
01568 | administration. In animals, convulsions following the administration of toxic doses of
01569 | tramadol hydrochloride tablets could be suppressed with barbiturates or
01570 | benzodiazepines but were increased with naloxone. Naloxone administration did not
01571 | change the lethality of an overdose in mice. Hemodialysis is not expected to be helpful in
01572 | an overdose because it removes less than 7% of the administered dose in a 4-hour
01573 | dialysis period.
01574 | Because the duration of opioid reversal is expected to be less than the duration of
01575 | action of tramadol in tramadol hydrochloride tablets, carefully monitor the patient until
01576 | spontaneous respiration is reliably reestablished. If the response to an opioid overdose
01577 | reversal agent is suboptimal or only brief in nature, administer additional reversal agent
01578 | as directed by the product's prescribing information.
01579 | In an individual physically dependent on opioids, administration of the recommended
01580 | dosage of the opioid overdose reversal agent will precipitate an acute withdrawal
01581 | syndrome. The severity of the withdrawal symptoms experienced will depend on the
01582 | degree of physical dependence and the dose of the reversal agent administered. If a
01583 | decision is made to treat serious respiratory depression in the physically dependent
01584 | patient, administration of the reversal agent should begin with care and by titration with
01585 | smaller than usual doses of the reversal agent.

01600 | 12 CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
01601 | 12.1 Mechanism of Action
01602 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets contains tramadol, an opioid agonist and inhibitor of
01603 | norepinephrine and serotonin re-uptake. Although the mode of action is not completely
01604 | understood, the analgesic effect of tramadol is believed to be due to both binding to μ-
01605 | opioid receptors and weak inhibition of re-uptake of norepinephrine and serotonin.
01606 | Opioid activity is due to both low affinity binding of the parent compound and higher
01607 | affinity binding of the O-demethylated metabolite M1 to μ-opioid receptors. In animal
01608 | models, M1 is up to 6 times more potent than tramadol in producing analgesia and 200
01609 | times more potent in μ- opioid binding. Tramadol-induced analgesia is only partially
01610 | antagonized by the opioid antagonist naloxone in several animal tests. The relative
01611 | contribution of both tramadol and M1 to human analgesia is dependent upon the plasma
01612 | concentrations of each compound [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.2)].
01613 | Analgesia in humans begins approximately within one hour after administration and
01614 | reaches a peak in approximately two to three hours.
01615 | 12.2 Pharmacodynamics
01616 | Effects on the Central Nervous System
01617 | Tramadol produces respiratory depression by direct action on brain stem respiratory
01618 | centers. The respiratory depression involves a reduction in the responsiveness of the
01619 | brain stem respiratory centers to both increases in carbon dioxide tension and electrical
01620 | stimulation.
01621 | Tramadol administration may produce a constellation of symptoms including nausea and
01622 | vomiting, dizziness, and somnolence.
01623 | Tramadol causes miosis, even in total darkness. Pinpoint pupils are a sign of opioid
01624 | 
01625 | overdose but are not pathognomonic (e.g., pontine lesions of hemorrhagic or ischemic
01626 | origins may produce similar findings). Marked mydriasis rather than miosis may be seen
01627 | due to hypoxia in overdose situations.
01628 | Effects on the Gastrointestinal Tract and Other Smooth Muscle
01629 | Tramadol causes a reduction in motility associated with an increase in smooth muscle
01630 | tone in the antrum of the stomach and duodenum. Digestion of food in the small
01631 | intestine is delayed and propulsive contractions are decreased. Propulsive peristaltic
01632 | waves in the colon are decreased, while tone may be increased to the point of spasm
01633 | resulting in constipation. Other opioid-induced effects may include a reduction in biliary
01634 | and pancreatic secretions, spasm of sphincter of Oddi, transient elevations in serum
01635 | amylase, and opioid-induced esophageal dysfunction (OIED).
01636 | Effects on the Cardiovascular System
01637 | Tramadol produces peripheral vasodilation, which may result in orthostatic hypotension
01638 | or syncope. Manifestations of peripheral vasodilation may include pruritus, flushing, red
01639 | eyes, sweating and/or orthostatic hypotension.
01640 | The effect of oral tramadol on the QTcF interval was evaluated in a double-blind,
01641 | randomized, four-way crossover, placebo- and positive- (moxifloxacin) controlled study
01642 | in 68 adult male and female healthy subjects. At a 600 mg/day dose (1.5-fold the
01643 | maximum immediate-release daily dose), the study demonstrated no significant effect
01644 | on the QTcF interval.
01645 | Effects on the Endocrine System
01646 | Opioids inhibit the secretion of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), cortisol, and
01647 | luteinizing hormone (LH) in humans. They also stimulate prolactin, growth hormone (GH)
01648 | secretion, and pancreatic secretion of insulin and glucagon [see Warnings and
01649 | Precautions (5.13); Adverse Reactions (6)].
01650 | Use of opioids for an extended period of time may influence the hypothalamic-pituitary-
01651 | gonadal axis, leading to androgen deficiency that may manifest as low libido, impotence,
01652 | erectile dysfunction, amenorrhea, or infertility. The causal role of opioids in the clinical
01653 | syndrome of hypogonadism is unknown because the various medical, physical, lifestyle,
01654 | and psychological stressors that may influence gonadal hormone levels have not been
01655 | adequately controlled for in studies conducted to date [see Adverse Reactions (6)].
01656 | Effects on the Immune System
01657 | Opioids have been shown to have a variety of effects on components of the immune
01658 | system in in vitro and animal models. The clinical significance of these findings is
01659 | unknown. Overall, the effects of opioids appear to be modestly immunosuppressive.
01660 | Concentration–Efficacy Relationships
01661 | The minimum effective analgesic concentration will vary widely among patients, especially
01662 | among patients who have been previously treated with extended-release agonists
01663 | opioid. The minimum effective analgesic concentration of tramadol for any individual
01664 | patient may increase over time due to an increase in pain, the development of a new
01665 | pain syndrome and/or the development of analgesic tolerance [see Dosage and
01666 | Administration (2)].
01667 | Concentration–Adverse Reaction Relationships
01668 | 
01669 | There is a relationship between increasing tramadol plasma concentration and increasing
01670 | frequency of dose-related opioid adverse reactions such as nausea, vomiting, CNS
01671 | effects, and respiratory depression. In opioid-tolerant patients, the situation may be
01672 | altered by the development of tolerance to opioid-related adverse reactions [see Dosage
01673 | and Administration (2)].

01833 | cytochrome P-450. These individuals are "poor metabolizers" of debrisoquine,
01834 | dextromethorphan, tricyclic antidepressants, among other drugs. Based on a population
01835 | PK analysis of Phase 1 studies in healthy subjects, concentrations of tramadol were
01836 | approximately 20% higher in "poor metabolizers" versus "extensive metabolizers", while
01837 | M1 concentrations were 40% lower. Concomitant therapy with inhibitors of CYP2D6
01838 | such as fluoxetine, paroxetine and quinidine could result in significant drug interactions.
01839 | In vitro drug interaction studies in human liver microsomes indicate that inhibitors of
01840 | CYP2D6 such as fluoxetine and its metabolite norfluoxetine, amitriptyline and quinidine
01841 | inhibit the metabolism of tramadol to various degrees, suggesting that concomitant
01842 | administration of these compounds could result in increases in tramadol concentrations
01843 | and decreased concentrations of M1. The full pharmacological impact of these
01844 | alterations in terms of either efficacy or safety is unknown. Concomitant use of
01845 | serotonin re-uptake inhibitors and MAO inhibitors may enhance the risk of adverse
01846 | events, including seizure and serotonin syndrome [see Warnings and Precautions (5.9)
01847 | and Drug Interactions (7)].
01848 | Excretion
01849 | Tramadol metabolites are eliminated primarily by the kidneys. Approximately 30% of the
01850 | dose is excreted in the urine as unchanged drug, whereas 60% of the dose is excreted
01851 | as metabolites. The remainder is excreted either as unidentified or as unextractable
01852 | metabolites.
01853 | Special Populations
01854 | Hepatic Impairment
01855 | Metabolism of tramadol and M1 is reduced in patients with severe hepatic impairment
01856 | based on a study in patients with advanced cirrhosis of the liver, resulting in both a
01857 | larger area under the concentration time curve for tramadol and longer tramadol and M1
01858 | elimination half-lives (13 hrs. for tramadol and 19 hrs. for M1). In patients with severe
01859 | hepatic impairment, adjustment of the dosing regimen is recommended [see Dosage and
01860 | Administration (2)].
01861 | Renal Impairment
01862 | Impaired renal function results in a decreased rate and extent of excretion of tramadol
01863 | and its active metabolite, M1. In patients with creatinine clearances of less than 30
01864 | mL/min, adjustment of the dosing regimen is recommended [see Dosage and
01865 | Administration (2)]. The total amount of tramadol and M1 removed during a 4-hour
01866 | dialysis period is less than 7% of the administered dose.
01867 | Age: Geriatric
01868 | Healthy elderly subjects aged 65 to 75 years have plasma tramadol concentrations and
01869 | elimination half-lives comparable to those observed in healthy subjects less than 65
01870 | years of age. In subjects over 75 years, maximum serum concentrations are elevated
01871 | 
01872 | (208 vs. 162 ng/mL) and the elimination half-life is prolonged (7 vs. 6 hours) compared
01873 | to subjects 65 to 75 years of age. Adjustment of the daily dose is recommended for
01874 | patients older than 75 years [see Dosage and Administration (2.3)].
01875 | Sex
01876 | The absolute bioavailability of tramadol was 73% in males and 79% in females. The
01877 | plasma clearance was 6.4 mL/min/kg in males and 5.7 mL/min/kg in females following a
01878 | 100 mg IV dose of tramadol. Following a single oral dose, and after adjusting for body
01879 | weight, females had a 12% higher peak tramadol concentration and a 35% higher area
01880 | under the concentration-time curve compared to males. The clinical significance of this
01881 | difference is unknown.
01882 | Poor / Extensive Metabolizers, CYP2D6
01883 | The formation of the active metabolite, M1, is mediated by CYP2D6, a polymorphic
01884 | enzyme. Approximately 7% of the population has reduced activity of the CYP2D6
01885 | isoenzyme of cytochrome P450 metabolizing enzyme system. These individuals are
01886 | "poor metabolizers" of debrisoquine, dextromethorphan and tricyclic antidepressants,
01887 | among other drugs. Based on a population PK analysis of Phase 1 studies with IR tablets
01888 | in healthy subjects, concentrations of tramadol were approximately 20% higher in "poor

01890 | 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY
01891 | 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
01892 | Carcinogenesis
01893 | A slight, but statistically significant, increase in two common murine tumors, pulmonary
01894 | and hepatic, was observed in an NMRI mouse carcinogenicity study, particularly in aged
01895 | mice. Mice were dosed orally up to 30 mg/kg in the drinking water (0.36 times the
01896 | MRHD) for approximately two years, although the study was not done with the
01897 | Maximum Tolerated Dose. This finding is not believed to suggest risk in humans. No
01898 | evidence of carcinogenicity was noted in a rat 2-year carcinogenicity study testing oral
01899 | doses of up to 30 mg/kg in the drinking water, 0.73 times the MRHD.
01900 | Mutagenesis
01901 | Tramadol was mutagenic in the presence of metabolic activation in the mouse lymphoma
01902 | assay. Tramadol was not mutagenic in the in vitro bacterial reverse mutation assay using
01903 | Salmonella and E. coli (Ames), the mouse lymphoma assay in the absence of metabolic
01904 | activation, the in vitro chromosomal aberration assay, or the in vivo micronucleus assay
01905 | in bone marrow.
01906 | Impairment of Fertility
01907 | No effects on fertility were observed for tramadol at oral dose levels up to 50 mg/kg in
01908 | male rats and 75 mg/kg in female rats. These dosages are 1.2 and 1.8 times the
01909 | maximum recommended human daily dose based on body surface area, respectively.
01910 | 14 CLINICAL STUDIES
01911 | 
01912 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets has been given in single oral doses of 50, 75 and 100 mg
01913 | to patients with pain following surgical procedures and pain following oral surgery
01914 | (extraction of impacted molars).
01915 | In single-dose models of pain following oral surgery, pain relief was demonstrated in
01916 | some patients at doses of 50 mg and 75 mg. A dose of 100 mg tramadol hydrochloride
01917 | tablets tended to provide analgesia superior to codeine sulfate 60 mg, but it was not as
01918 | effective as the combination of aspirin 650 mg with codeine phosphate 60 mg.
01919 | Tramadol hydrochloride tablets has been studied in three long-term controlled trials
01920 | involving a total of 820 patients, with 530 patients receiving tramadol hydrochloride
01921 | tablets. Patients with a variety of chronic painful conditions were studied in double-blind
01922 | trials of one to three months duration. Average daily doses of approximately 250 mg of
01923 | tramadol hydrochloride tablets in divided doses were generally comparable to five doses
01924 | of acetaminophen 300 mg with codeine phosphate 30 mg (TYLENOL with Codeine #3)
01925 | daily, five doses of aspirin 325 mg with codeine phosphate 30 mg daily, or two to three
01926 | doses of acetaminophen 500 mg with oxycodone hydrochloride 5 mg (TYLOX) daily.
01927 | Titration Trials
01928 | In a randomized, blinded clinical study with 129 to 132 patients per group, a 10-day
01929 | titration to a daily tramadol hydrochloride tablets dose of 200 mg (50 mg four times per
01930 | day), attained in 50 mg increments every 3 days, was found to result in fewer
01931 | discontinuations due to dizziness or vertigo than titration over only 4 days or no
01932 | titration. In a second study with 54 to 59 patients per group, patients who had nausea
01933 | or vomiting when titrated over 4 days were randomized to re-initiate tramadol
01934 | hydrochloride tablets therapy using slower titration rates.
01935 | A 16-day titration schedule, starting with 25 mg every morning and using additional
01936 | doses in 25 mg increments every third day to 100 mg/day (25 mg four times per day),
01937 | followed by 50 mg increments in the total daily dose every third day to 200 mg/day (50
01938 | mg four times per day), resulted in fewer discontinuations due to nausea or vomiting
01939 | and fewer discontinuations due to any cause than did a 10-day titration schedule.
