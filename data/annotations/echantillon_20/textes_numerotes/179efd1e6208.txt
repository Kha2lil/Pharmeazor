00294 | 1 INDICATIONS AND USAGE
00295 | Levofloxacin oral solution is indicated for the treatment of adults (≥18 years of age) with
00296 | mild, moderate, and severe infections caused by susceptible isolates of the designated
00297 | microorganisms in the conditions listed in this section.
00298 | 1.1 Nosocomial Pneumonia
00299 | Levofloxacin is indicated for the treatment of nosocomial pneumonia due to methicillin-
00300 | susceptible Staphylococcus aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Serratia marcescens,
00301 | Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, or Streptococcus
00302 | pneumoniae. Adjunctive therapy should be used as clinically indicated. Where
00303 | Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a documented or presumptive pathogen, combination
00304 | therapy with an anti-pseudomonal β-lactam is recommended [see Clinical Studies (14.1)].
00305 | 1.2 Community-Acquired Pneumonia: 7-14 day Treatment Regimen
00306 | 
00307 | Levofloxacin is indicated for the treatment of community-acquired pneumonia due to
00308 | methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae (including
00309 | multi- drug-resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae [MDRSP]), Haemophilus influenzae,
00310 | Haemophilus parainfluenzae, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Moraxella catarrhalis, Chlamydophila
00311 | pneumoniae, Legionella pneumophila, or Mycoplasma pneumoniae [see Dosage and
00312 | Administration (2.1) and Clinical Studies (14.2)]. 
00313 | MDRSP isolates are isolates resistant to two or more of the following antibacterials:
00314 | penicillin (MIC ≥2 mcg/mL), 2

00321 | Chlamydophila pneumoniae [see Dosage and Administration (2.1) and Clinical Studies

00333 | Levofloxacin is indicated for the treatment of chronic bacterial prostatitis due to
00334 | Escherichia coli, Enterococcus faecalis, or methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus
00335 | epidermidis [see Clinical Studies (14.6)].
00336 | 1.7 Inhalational Anthrax (Post-Exposure)
00337 | Levofloxacin is indicated for inhalational anthrax (post-exposure) to reduce the incidence
00338 | or progression of disease following exposure to aerosolized Bacillus anthracis. The
00339 | effectiveness of levofloxacin is based on plasma concentrations achieved in humans, a
00340 | surrogate endpoint reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit. Levofloxacin has not been
00341 | tested in humans for the post-exposure prevention of inhalation anthrax. The safety of
00342 | levofloxacin in adults for durations of therapy beyond 28 days or in pediatric patients for
00343 | durations of therapy beyond 14 days has not been studied. Prolonged levofloxacin
00344 | therapy should only be used when the benefit outweighs the risk [see Dosage and
00345 | Administration (2.1, 2.2) and Clinical Studies (14.9)].
00346 | 1.8 Plague
00347 | Levofloxacin is indicated for treatment of plague, including pneumonic and septicemic
00348 | nd
00349 | 
00350 | plague, due to Yersinia pestis (Y. pestis) and prophylaxis for plague in adults and
00351 | pediatric patients, 6 months of age and older. Efficacy studies of levofloxacin could not
00352 | be conducted in humans with plague for ethical and feasibility reasons. Therefore,
00353 | approval of this indication was based on an efficacy study conducted in animals [see
00354 | Dosage and Administration (2.1, 2.2) and Clinical Studies (14.10)].

00393 | 1.15 Usage
00394 | To reduce the development of drug-resistant bacteria and maintain the effectiveness of
00395 | levofloxacin and other antibacterial drugs, levofloxacin should be used only to treat or
00396 | prevent infections that are proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible
00397 | bacteria. When culture and susceptibility information are available, they should be
00398 | considered in selecting or modifying antibacterial therapy. In the absence of such data,
00399 | local epidemiology and susceptibility patterns may contribute to the empiric selection of
00400 | therapy.
00401 | Culture and susceptibility testing
00402 | Appropriate culture and susceptibility tests should be performed before treatment in
00403 | order to isolate and identify organisms causing the infection and to determine their
00404 | susceptibility to levofloxacin [see Microbiology (12.4)]. Therapy with levofloxacin may be
00405 | initiated before results of these tests are known; once results become available,
00406 | appropriate therapy should be selected.
00407 | As with other drugs in this class, some isolates of Pseudomonas aeruginosa may
00408 | develop resistance fairly rapidly during treatment with levofloxacin. Culture and
00409 | susceptibility testing performed periodically during therapy will provide information about
00410 | the continued susceptibility of the pathogens to the antimicrobial agent and also the
00411 | possible emergence of bacterial resistance.

00661 | 5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
00662 | 5.1 Disabling and Potentially Irreversible Serious Adverse Reactions Including
00663 | Tendinitis and Tendon Rupture, Peripheral Neuropathy, and Central Nervous
00664 | System Effects
00665 | Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, have been associated with disabling and
00666 | potentially irreversible serious adverse reactions from different body systems that can
00667 | occur together in the same patient. Commonly seen adverse reactions include tendinitis,
00668 | tendon rupture, arthralgia, myalgia, peripheral neuropathy, and central nervous system
00669 | effects (hallucinations, anxiety, depression, insomnia, severe headaches, and
00670 | confusion). These reactions can occur within hours to weeks after starting levofloxacin.
00671 | Patients of any age or without pre-existing risk factors have experienced these adverse
00672 | reactions [see Warnings and Precautions (5.2, 5.3, 5.4)].
00673 | Discontinue levofloxacin immediately at the first signs or symptoms of any serious
00674 | adverse reaction. In addition, avoid the use of fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, in
00675 | patients who have experienced any of these serious adverse reactions associated with
00676 | fluoroquinolones.
00677 | 5.2 Tendinitis and Tendon Rupture
00678 | Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, have been associated with an increased risk of
00679 | tendinitis and tendon rupture in all ages [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1) and
00680 | Adverse Reactions (6.2)]. This adverse reaction most frequently involves the Achilles
00681 | tendon and has also been reported with the rotator cuff (the shoulder), the hand, the
00682 | biceps, the thumb, and other tendon sites. Tendinitis or tendon rupture can occur within
00683 | hours or days of starting levofloxacin or as long as several months after completion of
00684 | fluoroquinolone therapy. Tendinitis and tendon rupture can occur bilaterally.
00685 | The risk of developing fluoroquinolone-associated tendinitis and tendon rupture is
00686 | increased in patients over 60 years of age, in those taking corticosteroid drugs, and in
00687 | patients with kidney, heart or lung transplants. Other factors that may independently
00688 | increase the risk of tendon rupture include strenuous physical activity, renal failure, and
00689 | previous tendon disorders such as rheumatoid arthritis. Tendinitis and tendon rupture
00690 | have been reported in patients taking fluoroquinolones who do not have the above risk
00691 | factors. Discontinue levofloxacin immediately if the patient experiences pain, swelling,
00692 | inflammation or rupture of a tendon. Patients should be advised to rest at the first sign
00693 | of tendinitis or tendon rupture, and to contact their healthcare provider regarding
00694 | changing to a non-quinolone antimicrobial drug. Avoid levofloxacin in patients who have
00695 | a history of tendon disorders or tendon rupture [see Adverse Reactions (6.3); Patient
00696 | Counseling Information (17)].
00697 | 5.3 Peripheral Neuropathy
00698 | Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, have been associated with an increased risk of
00699 | peripheral neuropathy. Cases of sensory or sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy
00700 | affecting small and/or large axons resulting in paresthesias, hypoesthesias, dysesthesias
00701 | and weakness have been reported in patients receiving fluoroquinolones, including
00702 | levofloxacin. Symptoms may occur soon after initiation of levofloxacin and may be

00706 | y
00707 | y
00708 | irreversible in some patients [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1) and Adverse Reactions
00709 | (6.1, 6.2)].
00710 | Discontinue levofloxacin immediately if the patient experiences symptoms of neuropathy
00711 | including pain, burning, tingling, numbness, and/or weakness or other alterations of
00712 | sensation including light touch, pain, temperature, position sense, and vibratory
00713 | sensation. Avoid fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, in patients who have
00714 | previously experienced peripheral neuropathy [see Adverse Reactions (6), Patient
00715 | Counseling Information (17)].
00716 | 5.4 Central Nervous System Effects
00717 | Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, have been associated with an increased risk of
00718 | central nervous system (CNS) effects, including convulsions, toxic psychoses, increased
00719 | intracranial pressure (including pseudotumor cerebri). Fluoroquinolones may also cause
00720 | central nervous system stimulation which may lead to tremors, restlessness, anxiety,
00721 | lightheadedness, confusion, hallucinations, paranoia, depression, nightmares, and
00722 | insomnia. Suicidal thoughts, and attempted or completed suicide may also occur,
00723 | especially in patients with a medical history of depression, or an underlying risk factor
00724 | for depression. These reactions may occur following the first dose. If these reactions
00725 | occur in patients receiving levofloxacin, discontinue levofloxacin and institute appropriate
00726 | measures. As with other fluoroquinolones, levofloxacin should be used with caution in
00727 | patients with a known or suspected central nervous system (CNS) disorder that may
00728 | predispose them to seizures or lower the seizure threshold (e.g., severe cerebral
00729 | arteriosclerosis, epilepsy) or in the presence of other risk factors that may predispose
00730 | them to seizures or lower the seizure threshold (e.g., certain drug therapy, renal
00731 | dysfunction). [see Adverse Reactions (6); Drug Interactions (7.4, 7.5); Patient Counseling
00732 | Information (17)].
00733 | 5.5 Exacerbation of Myasthenia Gravis
00734 | Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, have neuromuscular blocking activity and may
00735 | exacerbate muscle weakness in patients with myasthenia gravis. Postmarketing serious
00736 | adverse reactions including deaths and requirement for ventilatory support, have been
00737 | associated with fluoroquinolone use in patients with myasthenia gravis. Avoid
00738 | levofloxacin in patients with a known history of myasthenia gravis [see Adverse
00739 | Reactions (6.3); Patient Counseling Information (17)].

00749 | allergic pneumonitis;
00750 | interstitial nephritis; acute renal insufficiency or failure;
00751 | hepatitis; jaundice; acute hepatic necrosis or failure;
00752 | anemia, including hemolytic and aplastic; thrombocytopenia, including thrombotic

00762 | Discontinue levofloxacin immediately at the first appearance of skin rash, jaundice, or
00763 | any other sign of hypersensitivity and institute supportive measures [see Adverse

00765 | 5.7 Hypersensitivity Reactions
00766 | Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity and/or anaphylactic reactions have been
00767 | reported in patients receiving therapy with fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin.
00768 | These reactions often occur following the first dose. Some reactions have been
00769 | accompanied by cardiovascular collapse, acute myocardial ischemia with or without
00770 | myocardial infarction, hypotension/shock, seizure, loss of consciousness, tingling,
00771 | angioedema (including tongue, laryngeal, throat, or facial edema/swelling), airway
00772 | obstruction (including bronchospasm, shortness of breath, and acute respiratory
00773 | distress), dyspnea, urticaria, itching, and other serious skin reactions. Levofloxacin
00774 | should be discontinued immediately at the first appearance of a skin rash or any other
00775 | sign of hypersensitivity. Serious acute hypersensitivity reactions may require treatment
00776 | with epinephrine and other resuscitative measures, including oxygen, intravenous fluids,
00777 | antihistamines, corticosteroids, pressor amines, and airway management, as clinically
00778 | indicated [see Adverse Reactions (6); Patient Counseling Information (17)].
00779 | 5.8 Hepatotoxicity
00780 | Post-marketing reports of severe hepatotoxicity (including acute hepatitis and fatal
00781 | events) have been received for patients treated with levofloxacin. No evidence of serious
00782 | drug-associated hepatotoxicity was detected in clinical trials of over 7,000 patients.
00783 | Severe hepatotoxicity generally occurred within 14 days of initiation of therapy and most
00784 | cases occurred within 6 days. Most cases of severe hepatotoxicity were not associated
00785 | with hypersensitivity [see Warnings and Precautions (5.6)]. The majority of fatal
00786 | hepatotoxicity reports occurred in patients 65 years of age or older and most were not
00787 | associated with hypersensitivity. Levofloxacin should be discontinued immediately if the
00788 | patient develops signs and symptoms of hepatitis [see Adverse Reactions (6); Patient
00789 | Counseling Information (17)].
00790 | 5.9 Risk of Aortic Aneurysm and Dissection
00791 | Epidemiologic studies report an increased rate of aortic aneurysm and dissection within
00792 | two months following use of fluoroquinolones, particularly in elderly patients. The cause
00793 | for the increased risk has not been identified. In patients with a known aortic aneurysm
00794 | or patients who are at greater risk for aortic aneurysms, reserve levofloxacin for use
00795 | only when there are no alternative antibacterial treatments available.
00796 | 5.10 Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea
00797 | Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD) has been reported with use of nearly all
00798 | antibacterial agents, including levofloxacin, and may range in severity from mild diarrhea
00799 | to fatal colitis. Treatment with antibacterial agents alters the normal flora of the colon
00800 | leading to overgrowth of C. difficile.
00801 | C. difficile produces toxins A and B which contribute to the development of CDAD.
00802 | 
00803 | Hypertoxin producing strains of C. difficile cause increased morbidity and mortality, as
00804 | these infections can be refractory to antimicrobial therapy and may require colectomy.
00805 | CDAD must be considered in all patients who present with diarrhea following antibiotic
00806 | use. Careful medical history is necessary since CDAD has been reported to occur over
00807 | two months after the administration of antibacterial agents.
00808 | If CDAD is suspected or confirmed, ongoing antibiotic use not directed against C. difficile
00809 | may need to be discontinued. Appropriate fluid and electrolyte management, protein
00810 | supplementation, antibiotic treatment of C. difficile, and surgical evaluation should be
00811 | instituted as clinically indicated [see Adverse Reactions (6.2), Patient Counseling
00812 | Information (17)].
00813 | 5.11 Prolongation of the QT Interval
00814 | Some fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, have been associated with prolongation
00815 | of the QT interval on the electrocardiogram and infrequent cases of arrhythmia. Rare
00816 | cases of torsade de pointes have been spontaneously reported during postmarketing
00817 | surveillance in patients receiving fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin. Levofloxacin
00818 | should be avoided in patients with known prolongation of the QT interval, patients with
00819 | uncorrected hypokalemia, and patients receiving Class IA (quinidine, procainamide), or
00820 | Class III (amiodarone, sotalol) antiarrhythmic agents. Elderly patients may be more
00821 | susceptible to drug-associated effects on the QT interval [see Adverse Reactions (6.3),
00822 | Use in Specific Populations (8.5), and Patient Counseling Information (17)].
00823 | 5.12 Musculoskeletal Disorders in Pediatric Patients and Arthropathic Effects
00824 | in Animals
00825 | Levofloxacin is indicated in pediatric patients (6 months of age and older) only for the
00826 | prevention of inhalational anthrax (post-exposure) and for plague [see Indications and
00827 | Usage (1.7, 1.8)]. An increased incidence of musculoskeletal disorders (arthralgia,
00828 | arthritis, tendinopathy, and gait abnormality) compared to controls has been observed
00829 | in pediatric patients receiving levofloxacin [see Use in Specific Populations (8.4)].
00830 | In immature rats and dogs, the oral and intravenous administration of levofloxacin
00831 | resulted in increased osteochondrosis. Histopathological examination of the weight-
00832 | bearing joints of immature dogs dosed with levofloxacin revealed persistent lesions of
00833 | the cartilage. Other fluoroquinolones also produce similar erosions in the weight-bearing
00834 | joints and other signs of arthropathy in immature animals of various species [see Animal
00835 | Toxicology and/or Pharmacology (13.2)].
00836 | 5.13 Blood Glucose Disturbances
00837 | As with other fluoroquinolones, disturbances of blood glucose, including symptomatic
00838 | hyper- and hypoglycemia, have been reported with levofloxacin, usually in diabetic
00839 | patients receiving concomitant treatment with an oral hypoglycemic agent (e.g.,
00840 | glyburide) or with insulin. In these patients, careful monitoring of blood glucose is
00841 | recommended. If a hypoglycemic reaction occurs in a patient being treated with
00842 | levofloxacin, levofloxacin should be discontinued and appropriate therapy should be
00843 | initiated immediately [see Adverse Reactions (6.2); Drug Interactions (7.3); Patient
00844 | Counseling Information (17)].
00845 | 5.14 Photosensitivity/Phototoxicity
00846 | 
00847 | Moderate to severe photosensitivity/phototoxicity reactions, the latter of which may
00848 | manifest as exaggerated sunburn reactions (e.g., burning, erythema, exudation,
00849 | vesicles, blistering, edema) involving areas exposed to light (typically the face, “V” area
00850 | of the neck, extensor surfaces of the forearms, dorsa of the hands), can be associated
00851 | with the use of fluoroquinolones after sun or UV light exposure. Therefore, excessive
00852 | exposure to these sources of light should be avoided. Drug therapy should be
00853 | discontinued if photosensitivity/phototoxicity occurs [see Adverse Reactions (6.3);
00854 | Patient Counseling Information (17)].
00855 | 5.15 Development of Drug Resistant Bacteria
00856 | Prescribing levofloxacin in the absence of a proven or strongly suspected bacterial
00857 | infection or a prophylactic indication is unlikely to provide benefit to the patient and
00858 | increases the risk of the development of drug-resistant bacteria [see Patient Counseling
00859 | Information (17)].

01158 | 7 DRUG INTERACTIONS
01159 | 7.1 Chelation Agents: Antacids, Sucralfate, Metal Cations, Multivitamins
01160 | Levofloxacin Oral Solution
01161 | While the chelation by divalent cations is less marked than with other fluoroquinolones,
01162 | concurrent administration of levofloxacin oral solution with antacids containing
01163 | magnesium, or aluminum, as well as sucralfate, metal cations such as iron, and
01164 | multivitamin preparations with zinc may interfere with the gastrointestinal absorption of
01165 | levofloxacin, resulting in systemic levels considerably lower than desired. Tablets with
01166 | antacids containing magnesium, aluminum, as well as sucralfate, metal cations such as
01167 | iron, and multivitamin preparations with zinc or didanosine may substantially interfere
01168 | with the gastrointestinal absorption of levofloxacin, resulting in systemic levels
01169 | considerably lower than desired. These agents should be taken at least two hours
01170 | before or two hours after oral levofloxacin administration.
01171 | 7.2 Warfarin
01172 | No significant effect of levofloxacin on the peak plasma concentrations, AUC, and other
01173 | disposition parameters for R- and S- warfarin was detected in a clinical study involving
01174 | healthy volunteers. Similarly, no apparent effect of warfarin on levofloxacin absorption
01175 | and disposition was observed. However, there have been reports during the
01176 | postmarketing experience in patients that levofloxacin enhances the effects of warfarin.
01177 | Elevations of the prothrombin time in the setting of concurrent warfarin and levofloxacin
01178 | use have been associated with episodes of bleeding. Prothrombin time, International
01179 | Normalized Ratio (INR), or other suitable anticoagulation tests should be closely
01180 | monitored if levofloxacin is administered concomitantly with warfarin. Patients should
01181 | also be monitored for evidence of bleeding [see Adverse Reactions (6.3); Patient
01182 | Counseling Information (17)].
01183 | 7.3 Antidiabetic Agents
01184 | Disturbances of blood glucose, including hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia, have been
01185 | reported in patients treated concomitantly with fluoroquinolones and an antidiabetic
01186 | agent. Therefore, careful monitoring of blood glucose is recommended when these
01187 | agents are co-administered [see Warnings and Precautions (5.13); Adverse Reactions
01188 |

01246 | 8.1 Pregnancy
01247 | Pregnancy Category C. Levofloxacin was not teratogenic in rats at oral doses as high as
01248 | 810 mg/kg/day which corresponds to 9.4 times the highest recommended human dose
01249 | based upon relative body surface area, or at intravenous doses as high as 160
01250 | mg/kg/day corresponding to 1.9 times the highest recommended human dose based
01251 | upon relative body surface area. The oral dose of 810 mg/kg/day to rats caused
01252 | decreased fetal body weight and increased fetal mortality. No teratogenicity was
01253 | observed when rabbits were dosed orally as high as 50 mg/kg/day which corresponds
01254 | to 1.1 times the highest recommended human dose based upon relative body surface
01255 | area, or when dosed intravenously as high as 25 mg/kg/day, corresponding to 0.5 times
01256 | the highest recommended human dose based upon relative body surface area.
01257 | There are, however, no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women.
01258 | Levofloxacin should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the
01259 | potential risk to the fetus.
01260 | 8.3 Nursing Mothers
01261 | Based on data on other fluoroquinolones and very limited data on levofloxacin, it can be
01262 | presumed that levofloxacin will be excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for
01263 | serious adverse reactions from levofloxacin in nursing infants, a decision should be
01264 | made whether to discontinue nursing or to discontinue the drug, taking into account the
01265 | importance of the drug to the mother.
01266 | 8.4 Pediatric Use
01267 | Quinolones, including levofloxacin, cause arthropathy and osteochondrosis in juvenile
01268 | animals of several species. [see Warnings and Precautions (5.12) and Animal Toxicology
01269 | and/or Pharmacology (13.2)].
01270 | Pharmacokinetics following intravenous administration
01271 | The pharmacokinetics of levofloxacin following a single intravenous dose were
01272 | investigated in pediatric patients ranging in age from six months to 16 years. Pediatric
01273 | patients cleared levofloxacin faster than adult patients resulting in lower plasma
01274 | exposures than adults for a given mg/kg dose [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.3) and
01275 | Clinical Studies (14.9)].
01276 | Inhalational Anthrax (Post-Exposure)
01277 | Levofloxacin is indicated in pediatric patients 6 months of age and older, for inhalational
01278 | anthrax (post-exposure). The risk-benefit assessment indicates that administration of
01279 | levofloxacin to pediatric patients is appropriate. The safety of levofloxacin in pediatric
01280 | patients treated for more than 14 days has not been studied [see Indications and Usage
01281 | (1.7), Dosage and Administration (2.2) and Clinical Studies (14.9)].
01282 | Plague
01283 | 
01284 | Levofloxacin is indicated in pediatric patients, 6 months of age and older, for treatment
01285 | of plague, including pneumonic and septicemic plague due to Yersinia pestis (Y. pestis)
01286 | and prophylaxis for plague. Efficacy studies of levofloxacin could not be conducted in
01287 | humans with pneumonic plague for ethical and feasibility reasons. Therefore, approval of
01288 | this indication was based on an efficacy study conducted in animals. The risk-benefit
01289 | assessment indicates that administration of levofloxacin to pediatric patients is
01290 | appropriate [see Indications and Usage (1.8), Dosage and Administration (2.2) and
01291 | Clinical Studies (14.10)].
01292 | Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients below the age of six months have not been
01293 | established.

01344 | 8.5 Geriatric Use
01345 | Geriatric patients are at increased risk for developing severe tendon disorders including
01346 | tendon rupture when being treated with a fluoroquinolone such as levofloxacin. This risk
01347 | is further increased in patients receiving concomitant corticosteroid therapy. Tendinitis
01348 | or tendon rupture can involve the Achilles, hand, shoulder, or other tendon sites and
01349 | can occur during or after completion of therapy; cases occurring up to several months
01350 | after fluoroquinolone treatment have been reported. Caution should be used when
01351 | prescribing levofloxacin to elderly patients especially those on corticosteroids. Patients
01352 | should be informed of this potential side effect and advised to discontinue levofloxacin
01353 | and contact their healthcare provider if any symptoms of tendinitis or tendon rupture
01354 | occur [see Boxed Warning; Warnings and Precautions (5.2); and Adverse Reactions
01355 | (6.3)].
01356 | In Phase 3 clinical trials, 1,945 levofloxacin-treated patients (26%) were ≥ 65 years of
01357 | age. Of these, 1,081 patients (14%) were between the ages of 65 and 74 and 864
01358 | patients (12%) were 75 years or older. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness
01359 | were observed between these subjects and younger subjects, but greater sensitivity of
01360 | some older individuals cannot be ruled out.
01361 | Severe, and sometimes fatal, cases of hepatotoxicity have been reported post-
01362 | marketing in association with levofloxacin. The majority of fatal hepatotoxicity reports
01363 | occurred in patients 65 years of age or older and most were not associated with
01364 | hypersensitivity. Levofloxacin should be discontinued immediately if the patient develops
01365 | signs and symptoms of hepatitis [see Warnings and Precautions (5.8)].
01366 | Epidemiologic studies report an increased rate of aortic aneurysm and dissection within
01367 | two months following use of fluoroquinolones, particularly in elderly patients [see
01368 | Warnings and Precautions (5.9)].
01369 | Elderly patients may be more susceptible to drug-associated effects on the QT interval.
01370 | Therefore, precaution should be taken when using levofloxacin with concomitant drugs
01371 | that can result in prolongation of the QT interval (e.g., Class IA or Class III
01372 | antiarrhythmics) or in patients with risk factors for torsade de pointes (e.g., known QT
01373 | prolongation, uncorrected hypokalemia) [see Warnings and Precautions (5.11)].
01374 | The pharmacokinetic properties of levofloxacin in younger adults and elderly adults do
01375 | not differ significantly when creatinine clearance is taken into consideration. However,
01376 | since the drug is known to be substantially excreted by the kidney, the risk of toxic
01377 | reactions to this drug may be greater in patients with impaired renal function. Because
01378 | elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function, care should be taken in
01379 | dose selection, and it may be useful to monitor renal function [see Clinical Pharmacology
01380 | (12.3)].
01381 | 8.6 Renal Impairment
01382 | Clearance of levofloxacin is substantially reduced and plasma elimination half-life is
01383 | 
01384 | substantially prolonged in patients with impaired renal function (creatinine clearance <
01385 | 50 mL/min), requiring dosage adjustment in such patients to avoid accumulation. Neither
01386 | hemodialysis nor continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) is effective in removal
01387 | of levofloxacin from the body, indicating that supplemental doses of levofloxacin are not
01388 | required following hemodialysis or CAPD [see Dosage and Administration (2.3)].
01389 | 8.7 Hepatic Impairment
01390 | Pharmacokinetic studies in hepatically impaired patients have not been conducted. Due
01391 | to the limited extent of levofloxacin metabolism, the pharmacokinetics of levofloxacin are
01392 | not expected to be affected by hepatic impairment.
01393 | 10 OVERDOSAGE
01394 | In the event of an acute overdosage, the stomach should be emptied. The patient
01395 | should be observed and appropriate hydration maintained. Levofloxacin is not efficiently
01396 | removed by hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis.
01397 | Levofloxacin exhibits a low potential for acute toxicity. Mice, rats, dogs and monkeys
01398 | exhibited the following clinical signs after receiving a single high dose of levofloxacin:
01399 | ataxia, ptosis, decreased locomotor activity, dyspnea, prostration, tremors, and
01400 | convulsions. Doses in excess of 1500 mg/kg orally and 250 mg/kg IV produced
01401 | significant mortality in rodents.

01783 | Absorption
01784 | Levofloxacin is rapidly and essentially completely absorbed after oral administration.
01785 | Peak plasma concentrations are usually attained one to two hours after oral dosing. The
01786 | absolute bioavailability of levofloxacin from a 500 mg tablet and a 750 mg tablet of
01787 | levofloxacin are both approximately 99%, demonstrating complete oral absorption of
01788 | levofloxacin. Following a single intravenous dose of levofloxacin to healthy volunteers,
01789 | the mean ± SD peak plasma concentration attained was 6.2 ± 1.0 mcg/mL after a 500
01790 | mg dose infused over 60 minutes and 11.5 ± 4.0 mcg/mL after a 750 mg dose infused
01791 | over 90 minutes. Levofloxacin oral solution and tablet formulations are bioequivalent.
01792 | Levofloxacin pharmacokinetics are linear and predictable after single and multiple oral or
01793 | IV dosing regimens. Steady-state conditions are reached within 48 hours following a 500
01794 | mg or 750 mg once-daily dosage regimen. The mean ± SD peak and trough plasma
01795 | concentrations attained following multiple once-daily oral dosage regimens were
01796 | approximately 5.7 ± 1.4 and 0.5 ± 0.2 mcg/mL after the 500 mg doses, and 8.6 ± 1.9
01797 | and 1.1 ± 0.4 mcg/mL after the 750 mg doses, respectively. The mean ± SD peak and
01798 | trough plasma concentrations attained following multiple once-daily IV regimens were
01799 | approximately 6.4 ± 0.8 and 0.6 ± 0.2 mcg/mL after the 500 mg doses, and 12.1 ± 4.1
01800 | and 1.3 ± 0.71 mcg/mL after the 750 mg doses, respectively. Oral administration of a
01801 | 500 mg dose of levofloxacin with food prolongs the time to peak concentration by
01802 | approximately 1 hour and decreases the peak concentration by approximately 14%
01803 | following tablet and approximately 25% following oral solution administration. Therefore,
01804 | levofloxacin tablets can be administered without regard to food. It is recommended that
01805 | levofloxacin oral solution be taken 1 hour before or 2 hours after eating.
01806 | The plasma concentration profile of levofloxacin after IV administration is similar and
01807 | comparable in extent of exposure (AUC) to that observed for levofloxacin tablets when
01808 | equal doses (mg/mg) are administered. Therefore, the oral and IV routes of
01809 | administration can be considered interchangeable (see Figure 2 and Figure 3).

01835 | Metabolism
01836 | Levofloxacin is stereochemically stable in plasma and urine and does not invert
01837 | metabolically to its enantiomer, D-ofloxacin. Levofloxacin undergoes limited metabolism
01838 | in humans and is primarily excreted as unchanged drug in the urine. Following oral
01839 | administration, approximately 87% of an administered dose was recovered as
01840 | unchanged drug in urine within 48 hours, whereas less than 4% of the dose was
01841 | recovered in feces in 72 hours. Less than 5% of an administered dose was recovered in
01842 | the urine as the desmethyl and N-oxide metabolites, the only metabolites identified in
01843 | humans. These metabolites have little relevant pharmacological activity.
01844 | Excretion
01845 | Levofloxacin is excreted largely as unchanged drug in the urine. The mean terminal
01846 | plasma elimination half-life of levofloxacin ranges from approximately 6 to 8 hours
01847 | following single or multiple doses of levofloxacin given orally or intravenously. The mean
01848 | apparent total body clearance and renal clearance range from approximately 144 to 226
01849 | mL/min and 96 to 142 mL/min, respectively. Renal clearance in excess of the glomerular
01850 | filtration rate suggests that tubular secretion of levofloxacin occurs in addition to its
01851 | glomerular filtration. Concomitant administration of either cimetidine or probenecid
01852 | results in approximately 24% and 35% reduction in the levofloxacin renal clearance,
01853 | respectively, indicating that secretion of levofloxacin occurs in the renal proximal tubule.
01854 | No levofloxacin crystals were found in any of the urine samples freshly collected from
01855 | subjects receiving levofloxacin.
01856 | Geriatric
01857 | There are no significant differences in levofloxacin pharmacokinetics between young and
01858 | elderly subjects when the subjects’ differences in creatinine clearance are taken into
01859 | consideration. Following a 500 mg oral dose of levofloxacin to healthy elderly subjects
01860 | (66-80 years of age), the mean terminal plasma elimination half-life of levofloxacin was
01861 | about 7.6 hours, as compared to approximately 6 hours in younger adults. The
01862 | difference was attributable to the variation in renal function status of the subjects and
01863 | was not believed to be clinically significant. Drug absorption appears to be unaffected by

01914 | 12.4 Microbiology
01915 | Mechanism of Action
01916 | Levofloxacin is the L-isomer of the racemate, ofloxacin, a quinolone antimicrobial agent.
01917 | The antibacterial activity of ofloxacin resides primarily in the L-isomer. The mechanism of
01918 | action of levofloxacin and other fluoroquinolone antimicrobials involves inhibition of
01919 | bacterial topoisomerase IV and DNA gyrase (both of which are type II topoisomerases),
01920 | enzymes required for DNA replication, transcription, repair and recombination.
01921 | Mechanism of Resistance
01922 | Fluoroquinolone resistance can arise through mutations in defined regions of DNA
01923 | gyrase or topoisomerase IV, termed the Quinolone-Resistance Determining Regions
01924 | (QRDRs), or through altered efflux.
01925 | Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, differ in chemical structure and mode of action
01926 | 
01927 | from aminoglycosides, macrolides and β-lactam antibiotics, including penicillins.
01928 | Fluoroquinolones may, therefore, be active against bacteria resistant to these
01929 | antimicrobials.
01930 | Resistance to levofloxacin due to spontaneous mutation in vitro is a rare occurrence
01931 | (range: 10
01932 |  to 10
01933 | ). Cross-resistance has been observed between levofloxacin and
01934 | some other fluoroquinolones, some microorganisms resistant to other fluoroquinolones
01935 | may be susceptible to levofloxacin.

02029 | 13.2 Animal Toxicology and/or Pharmacology
02030 | Levofloxacin and other quinolones have been shown to cause arthropathy in immature
02031 | animals of most species tested [see Warnings and Precautions (5.12)]. In immature
02032 | dogs (4–5 months old), oral doses of 10 mg/kg/day for 7 days and intravenous doses of
02033 | 4 mg/kg/day for 14 days of levofloxacin resulted in arthropathic lesions. Administration
02034 | at oral doses of 300 mg/kg/day for 7 days and intravenous doses of 60 mg/kg/day for 4
02035 | weeks produced arthropathy in juvenile rats. Three-month old beagle dogs dosed orally
02036 | with levofloxacin at 40 mg/kg/day exhibited clinically severe arthrotoxicity resulting in the
02037 | termination of dosing at Day 8 of a 14-day dosing routine. Slight musculoskeletal clinical
02038 | effects, in the absence of gross pathological or histopathological effects, resulted from

02046 | similar in magnitude to ofloxacin, but less phototoxicity than other quinolones.

02048 | are not formed in the bladder, being present only after micturition and are not

02713 | other symptoms of an allergic reaction.
02714 | Hepatotoxicity: Inform patients that severe hepatotoxicity (including acute hepatitis
02715 | and fatal events) has been reported in patients taking levofloxacin. Instruct patients
02716 | to inform their physician if they experience any signs or symptoms of liver injury
02717 | including: loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, fever, weakness, tiredness, right upper
02718 | quadrant tenderness, itching, yellowing of the skin and eyes, light colored bowel
02719 | movements or dark colored urine.
02720 | Aortic aneurysm and dissection: Inform patients to seek emergency medical care
02721 | if they experience sudden chest, stomach, or back pain.
02722 | Diarrhea: Diarrhea is a common problem caused by antibiotics which usually ends
02723 | when the antibiotic is discontinued. Sometimes after starting treatment with
02724 | antibiotics, patients can develop watery and bloody stools (with or without stomach
02725 | cramps and fever) even as late as two or more months after having taken the last
02726 | dose of the antibiotic. If this occurs, instruct patients to contact their physician as
02727 | soon as possible.
02728 | Prolongation of the QT Interval: Instruct patients to inform their physician of any
02729 | personal or family history of QT prolongation or proarrhythmic conditions such as
02730 | hypokalemia, bradycardia, or recent myocardial ischemia; if they are taking any Class
02731 | IA (quinidine, procainamide), or Class III (amiodarone, sotalol) antiarrhythmic agents.
02732 | Instruct patients to notify their physician if they have any symptoms of prolongation
02733 | of the QT interval, including prolonged heart palpitations or a loss of consciousness.
02734 | Musculoskeletal Disorders in Pediatric Patients: Instruct parents to inform
02735 | their child’s physician if the child has a history of joint-related problems before taking
02736 | this drug. Inform parents of pediatric patients to notify their child’s physician of any
02737 | joint-related problems that occur during or following levofloxacin therapy [see
02738 | Warnings and Precautions (5.12) and Use in Specific Populations (8.4)].
02739 | 
02740 | Photosensitivity/Phototoxicity: Inform patients that photosensitivity/phototoxicity
02741 | has been reported in patients receiving fluoroquinolones. Inform patients to minimize
02742 | or avoid exposure to natural or artificial sunlight (tanning beds or UVA/B treatment)
02743 | while taking fluoroquinolones. If patients need to be outdoors while using
02744 | fluoroquinolones, instruct them to wear loose-fitting clothes that protect skin from
02745 | sun exposure and discuss other sun protection measures with their physician. If a
02746 | sunburn-like reaction or skin eruption occurs, instruct patients to contact their
02747 | physician.
02748 | Antibacterial Resistance
02749 | Antibacterial drugs including levofloxacin should only be used to treat bacterial
02750 | infections. They do not treat viral infections (e.g., the common cold). When levofloxacin
02751 | is prescribed to treat a bacterial infection, patients should be told that although it is
02752 | common to feel better early in the course of therapy, the medication should be taken
02753 | exactly as directed. Skipping doses or not completing the full course of therapy may (1)
02754 | decrease the effectiveness of the immediate treatment and (2) increase the likelihood
02755 | that bacteria will develop resistance and will not be treatable by levofloxacin or other
02756 | antibacterial drugs in the future.
02757 | Administration with Food, Fluids, and Concomitant Medications
02758 | Patients should be informed that levofloxacin oral solution should be taken 1 hour
02759 | before or 2 hours after eating. The oral solution should be taken at the same time each
02760 | day.
02761 | Patients should drink fluids liberally while taking levofloxacin to avoid formation of a
02762 | highly concentrated urine and crystal formation in the urine.
02763 | Antacids containing magnesium, or aluminum, as well as sucralfate, metal cations such
02764 | as iron, and multivitamin preparations with zinc or didanosine should be taken at least
02765 | two hours before or two hours after oral levofloxacin administration.
02766 | Drug Interactions with Insulin, Oral Hypoglycemic Agents, and Warfarin 
02767 | Patients should be informed that if they are diabetic and are being treated with insulin or
02768 | an oral hypoglycemic agent and a hypoglycemic reaction occurs, they should
02769 | discontinue levofloxacin and consult a physician.
02770 | Patients should be informed that concurrent administration of warfarin and levofloxacin
02771 | has been associated with increases of the International Normalized Ratio (INR) or
02772 | prothrombin time and clinical episodes of bleeding. Patients should notify their physician
02773 | if they are taking warfarin, be monitored for evidence of bleeding, and also have their
02774 | anticoagulation tests closely monitored while taking warfarin concomitantly.
02775 | Plague and Anthrax Studies
02776 | Patients given levofloxacin for these conditions should be informed that efficacy studies
02777 | could not be conducted in humans for ethical and feasibility reasons. Therefore,
02778 | approval for these conditions was based on efficacy studies conducted in animals.

02792 | What is the most important information I should know about levofloxacin?
02793 | Levofloxacin, a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, can cause serious side effects.
02794 | Some of these serious side effects can happen at the same time and could
02795 | result in death.
02796 | If you have any of the following serious side effects while you take levofloxacin, you
02797 | should stop taking levofloxacin immediately and get medical help right away.
02798 | 1. Tendon rupture or swelling of the tendon (tendinitis).
02799 | Tendon problems can happen in people of all ages who take levofloxacin.
02800 | Tendons are tough cords of tissue that connect muscles to bones.
02801 | Some tendon problems include pain, swelling, tears, and swelling of tendons including
02802 | the back of the ankle (Achilles), shoulder, hand, or other tendon sites.
02803 | The risk of getting tendon problems while you take levofloxacin is higher if you:
02804 | are over 60 years of age
02805 | are taking steroids (corticosteroids)
02806 | have had a kidney, heart or lung transplant.
02807 | Tendon problems can happen in people who do not have the above risk factors when
02808 | they take levofloxacin.
02809 | Other reasons that can increase your risk of tendon problems can include:
02810 | physical activity or exercise
02811 | kidney failure
02812 | tendon problems in the past, such as in people with rheumatoid arthritis (RA)
02813 | Stop taking levofloxacin immediately and get medical help right away at the first sign
02814 | of tendon pain, swelling or inflammation. Avoid exercise and using the affected area.
02815 | The most common area of pain and swelling is the Achilles tendon at the back of your
02816 | ankle. This can also happen with other tendons. You may need a different antibiotic that
02817 | is not a fluoroquinolone to treat your infection.
02818 | Tendon rupture can happen while you are taking or after you have finished taking
02819 | levofloxacin. Tendon ruptures can happen within hours or days of taking levofloxacin
02820 | and have happened up to several months after people have finished taking their
02821 | fluoroquinolone.
02822 | Stop taking levofloxacin immediately and get medical help right away if you get any of
02823 | 
02824 | the following signs or symptoms of a tendon rupture:
02825 | hear or feel a snap or pop in a tendon area
02826 | bruising right after an injury in a tendon area
02827 | unable to move the affected area or bear weight
02828 | 2. Changes in sensation and possible nerve damage (Peripheral Neuropathy).
02829 | Damage to the nerves in arms, hands, legs, or feet can happen in people who take
02830 | fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin. Stop taking levofloxacin immediately and talk to
02831 | your healthcare provider right away if you get any of the following symptoms of
02832 | peripheral neuropathy in your arms, hands, legs, or feet:
02833 | pain
02834 | burning
02835 | tingling
02836 | numbness
02837 | weakness
02838 | The nerve damage may be permanent.
02839 | 3. Central Nervous System (CNS) effects. Seizures have been reported in people
02840 | who take fluoroquinolone antibacterial medicines, including levofloxacin. Tell your
02841 | healthcare provider if you have a history of seizures before you start taking levofloxacin.
02842 | CNS side effects may happen as soon as after taking the first dose of levofloxacin. Stop
02843 | taking levofloxacin immediately and talk to your healthcare provider right away if you get
02844 | any of these side effects, or changes in mood or behavior:
02845 | seizures
02846 | hear voices, see things, or sense things that are not there (hallucinations)
02847 | feel restless
02848 | tremors
02849 | feel anxious or nervous
02850 | confusion
02851 | depression
02852 | trouble sleeping
02853 | nightmares
02854 | feel lightheaded or dizzy
02855 | feel more suspicious (paranoia)
02856 | suicidal thoughts or acts
02857 | headaches that will not go away, with or without blurred vision
02858 | 4. Worsening of myasthenia gravis (a problem that causes muscle
02859 | weakness). Fluoroquinolones like levofloxacin may cause worsening of myasthenia
02860 | gravis symptoms, including muscle weakness and breathing problems. Tell your
02861 | healthcare provider if you have a history of myasthenia gravis before you start taking
02862 | levofloxacin. Call your healthcare provider right away if you have any worsening muscle
02863 | weakness or breathing problems.
02864 | What is levofloxacin?
02865 | Levofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic medicine used in adults age 18 years or older
02866 | to treat certain infections caused by certain germs called bacteria. These bacterial
02867 | infections include:
02868 | 
02869 | nosocomial pneumonia
02870 | community acquired pneumonia
02871 | acute sinus infection
02872 | acute worsening of chronic bronchitis
02873 | skin infections, complicated and uncomplicated
02874 | chronic prostate infection
02875 | urinary tract infections, complicated and uncomplicated
02876 | acute kidney infection (pyelonephritis)
02877 | inhalational anthrax
02878 | plague
02879 | Studies of levofloxacin for use in the treatment of plague and anthrax were done in
02880 | animals only, because plague and anthrax could not be studied in people.
02881 | Levofloxacin should not be used in patients with uncomplicated urinary tract infections,
02882 | acute bacterial exacerbation of chronic bronchitis, or acute bacterial sinusitis if there are
02883 | other treatment options available.
02884 | Levofloxacin is also used to treat children who are 6 months of age or older and may
02885 | have breathed in anthrax germs, have plague, or been exposed to plague germs.
02886 | It is not known if levofloxacin is safe and effective in children under 6 months of age.
02887 | The safety and effectiveness in children treated with levofloxacin for more than 14 days
02888 | is not known.
02889 | Who should not take levofloxacin?
02890 | Do not take levofloxacin if you have ever had a severe allergic reaction to an
02891 | antibiotic known as a fluoroquinolone, or if you are allergic to levofloxacin or any of the
02892 | ingredients in levofloxacin. See the end of this leaflet for a complete list of ingredients in
02893 | levofloxacin.

03002 | Allergic reactions can happen in people taking fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin,
03003 | even after only 1 dose. Stop taking levofloxacin and get emergency medical help right

03005 | hives

03007 | swelling of the lips, tongue, face
03008 | throat tightness, hoarseness
03009 | rapid heartbeat
03010 | faint
03011 | skin rash
03012 | Skin rash may happen in people taking levofloxacin, even after only 1 dose. Stop taking
03013 | levofloxacin at the first sign of a skin rash and immediately call your healthcare provider.
03014 | Skin rash may be a sign of a more serious reaction to levofloxacin.
03015 | Liver damage (hepatotoxicity): Hepatotoxicity can happen in people who take
03016 | levofloxacin. Call your healthcare provider right away if you have unexplained
03017 | symptoms such as:
03018 | 
03019 | y
03020 | p
03021 | nausea or vomiting
03022 | stomach pain
03023 | fever
03024 | weakness
03025 | abdominal pain or tenderness
03026 | itching
03027 | unusual tiredness
03028 | loss of appetite
03029 | light colored bowel movements
03030 | dark colored urine
03031 | yellowing of your skin or the whites of your eyes
03032 | Stop taking levofloxacin and tell your healthcare provider right away if you have
03033 | yellowing of your skin or white part of your eyes, or if you have dark urine. These can be
03034 | signs of a serious reaction to levofloxacin (a liver problem).
03035 | Aortic aneurysm and dissection
03036 | Tell your healthcare provider if you have ever been told that you have an aortic
03037 | aneurysm, a swelling of the large artery that carries blood from the heart to the body.

03041 | Pseudomembranous colitis can happen with many antibiotics, including levofloxacin.

03044 | Pseudomembranous colitis can happen 2 or more months after you have finished
03045 | your antibiotic.

03047 | Tell your healthcare provider right away if you have a change in your heart beat (a

03049 | problem known as prolongation of the QT interval. This condition can cause an
03050 | abnormal heartbeat and can be very dangerous. The chances of this happening are
03051 | higher in people:
03052 | who are elderly
03053 | with a family history of prolonged QT interval
03054 | with low blood potassium (hypokalemia)
03055 | who take certain medicines to control heart rhythm (antiarrhythmics)
03056 | Joint Problems 
03057 | Increased chance of problems with joints and tissues around joints in children can
03058 | happen. Tell your child's healthcare provider if your child has any joint problems
03059 | during or after treatment with levofloxacin.
03060 | Changes in blood sugar 
03061 | People who take levofloxacin and other fluoroquinolone medicines with oral anti-
03062 | diabetes medicines or with insulin can get low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) and high
03063 | blood sugar (hyperglycemia). Follow your healthcare provider's instructions for how

03065 | while taking levofloxacin, stop taking levofloxacin and call your healthcare provider
03066 | right away. Your antibiotic medicine may need to be changed.

03071 | nausea
03072 | headache
03073 | diarrhea
03074 | insomnia
03075 | constipation
03076 | dizziness
