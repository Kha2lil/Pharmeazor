00106 | CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
00107 | Mechanism of Action
00108 | Codeine is an opioid agonist relatively selective for the mu-opioid receptor, but with a much weaker
00109 | affinity than morphine. The analgesic properties of codeine have been speculated to come from its
00110 | conversion to morphine, although the exact mechanism of analgesic action remains unknown.
00111 | The precise mechanism of the analgesic properties of acetaminophen is not established but is thought to
00112 | involve central actions.
00113 | Pharmacodynamics
00114 | Effects on the Central Nervous System
00115 | Codeine produces respiratory depression by direct action on brain stem respiratory centers. The
00116 | respiratory depression involves a reduction in the responsiveness of the brain stem respiratory centers
00117 | to both increases in carbon dioxide tension and electrical stimulation.
00118 | Codeine causes miosis, even in total darkness. Pinpoint pupils are a sign of opioid overdose but are not
00119 | pathognomonic (e.g., pontine lesions of hemorrhagic or ischemic origins may produce similar
00120 | findings). Marked mydriasis rather than miosis may be seen due to hypoxia in overdose situations.
00121 | Effects on the Gastrointestinal Tract and Other Smooth Muscle
00122 | Codeine causes a reduction in motility associated with an increase in smooth muscle tone in the antrum
00123 | of the stomach and duodenum. Digestion of food in the small intestine is delayed and propulsive
00124 | contractions are decreased. Propulsive peristaltic waves in the colon are decreased, while tone may be
00125 | increased to the point of spasm, resulting in constipation. Other opioid-induced effects may include a
00126 | reduction in biliary and pancreatic secretions, spasm of sphincter of Oddi, and transient elevations in
00127 | serum amylase.
00128 | Effects on the Cardiovascular System
00129 | Codeine produces peripheral vasodilation which may result in orthostatic hypotension or syncope.
00130 | Manifestations of histamine release and/or peripheral vasodilation may include pruritus, flushing, red
00131 | eyes, sweating, and/or orthostatic hypotension.
00132 | Effects on the Endocrine System
00133 | Opioids inhibit the secretion of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), cortisol, and luteinizing
00134 | hormone (LH) in humans [see ADVERSE REACTIONS]. They also stimulate prolactin, growth
00135 | hormone (GH) secretion, and pancreatic secretion of insulin and glucagon.
00136 | 
00137 | Chronic use of opioids may influence the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, leading to androgen
00138 | deficiency that may manifest as low libido, impotence, erectile dysfunction, amenorrhea, or infertility.
00139 | The causal role of opioids in the clinical syndrome of hypogonadism is unknown because the various
00140 | medical, physical, lifestyle, and psychological stressors that may influence gonadal hormone levels
00141 | have not been adequately controlled for in studies conducted to date [see ADVERSE REACTIONS].
00142 | Effects on the Immune System
00143 | Opioids have been shown to have a variety of effects on components of the immune system. The clinical
00144 | significance of these findings is unknown. Overall, the effects of opioids appear to be modestly
00145 | immunosuppressive.
00146 | Concentration–Efficacy Relationships
00147 | The minimum effective analgesic concentration will vary widely among patients, especially among
00148 | patients who have been previously treated with potent agonist opioids. The minimum effective analgesic
00149 | concentration of codeine for any individual patient may increase over time due to an increase in pain, the
00150 | development of a new pain syndrome, and/or the development of analgesic tolerance [see DOSAGE
00151 | AND ADMINISTRATION].
00152 | Concentration–Adverse Reaction Relationships
00153 | There is a relationship between increasing codeine plasma concentration and increasing frequency of
00154 | dose-related opioid adverse reactions such as nausea, vomiting, CNS effects, and respiratory
00155 | depression. In opioid-tolerant patients, the situation may be altered by the development of tolerance to
00156 | opioid-related adverse reactions [see DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].
00157 | Pharmacokinetics
00158 | The behavior of the individual components is described below.
00159 | Codeine
00160 | Codeine is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. It is rapidly distributed from the
00161 | intravascular spaces to the various body tissues, with preferential uptake by parenchymatous organs
00162 | such as the liver, spleen, and kidney. Codeine crosses the blood-brain barrier and is found in fetal tissue
00163 | and breast milk. The plasma concentration does not correlate with brain concentration or relief of pain.
00164 | Codeine is about 7 to 25% bound to plasma proteins and does not accumulate in body tissues.
00165 | About 70 to 80% of the administered dose of codeine is metabolized by conjugation with glucuronic
00166 | acid to codeine-6-glucuronide (C6G) and via O-demethylation to morphine (about 5 to 10%) and N-
00167 | demethylation to norcodeine (about 10%) respectively. UDP-glucuronosyltransferase (UGT) 2B7 and
00168 | 2B4 are the major enzymes mediating glucuronidation of codeine to C6G. Cytochrome P450 2D6 is the
00169 | major enzyme responsible for conversion of codeine to morphine and P450 3A4 is the major enzyme
00170 | mediating conversion of codeine to norcodeine. Morphine and norcodeine are further metabolized by
00171 | conjugation with glucuronic acid. The glucuronide metabolites of morphine are morphine-3-
00172 | glucuronide (M3G) and morphine-6-glucuronide (M6G). Morphine and M6G are known to have
00173 | analgesic activity in humans. The analgesic activity of C6G in humans is unknown. Norcodeine and M3G
00174 | are generally not considered to possess analgesic properties.
00175 | The plasma half-life is about 2.9 hours. The elimination of codeine is primarily via the kidneys, and
00176 | about 90% of an oral dose is excreted by the kidneys within 24 hours of dosing. The urinary secretion
00177 | products consist of free and glucuronide conjugated codeine (about 70%), free and conjugated
00178 | norcodeine (about 10%), free and conjugated morphine (about 10%), normorphine (4%), and
00179 | hydrocodone (1%). The remainder of the dose is excreted in the feces.
00180 | At therapeutic doses, the analgesic effect reaches a peak within 2 hours and persists between 4 and 6
00181 | hours.
00182 | Acetaminophen
00183 | 
00184 | Acetaminophen is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and is distributed throughout most
00185 | body tissues. A small fraction (10 to 25%) of acetaminophen is bound to plasma proteins. The plasma
00186 | half-life is 1.25 to 3 hours, but may be increased by liver damage and following overdosage.
00187 | Elimination of acetaminophen is principally by liver metabolism (conjugation) and subsequent renal
00188 | excretion of metabolites. Acetaminophen is primarily metabolized in the liver by first-order kinetics and
00189 | involves three principal separate pathways: conjugation with glucuronide; conjugation with sulfate; and
00190 | oxidation via the cytochrome, P450-dependent, mixed-function oxidase enzyme pathway to form a
00191 | reactive intermediate metabolite, which conjugates with glutathione and is then further metabolized to
00192 | form cysteine and mercapturic acid conjugates. The principal cytochrome P450 isoenzyme involved
00193 | appears to be CYP2E1, with CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 as additional pathways. Approximately 85% of an
00194 | oral dose appears in the urine within 24 hours of administration, most as the glucuronide conjugate, with
00195 | small amounts of other conjugates and unchanged drug.
00196 | See OVERDOSAGE for toxicity information.
00197 | INDICATIONS AND USAGE
00198 | Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are indicated for the management of mild to moderate
00199 | pain, where treatment with an opioid is appropriate and for which alternative treatments are inadequate.

00220 | WARNINGS
00221 | Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse
00222 | Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets contain codeine. Codeine in combination with
00223 | acetaminophen, is a Schedule III controlled substance. As an opioid, acetaminophen and codeine
00224 | phosphate tablets expose users to the risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse [see DRUG ABUSE AND
00225 | DEPENDENCE].
00226 | Although the risk of addiction in any individual is unknown, it can occur in patients appropriately
00227 | 
00228 | prescribed acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets. Addiction can occur at recommended dosages
00229 | and if the drug is misused or abused.
00230 | Assess each patient’s risk for opioid addiction, abuse, or misuse prior to prescribing acetaminophen
00231 | and codeine phosphate tablets, and monitor all patients receiving acetaminophen and codeine phosphate
00232 | tablets for the development of these behaviors and conditions. Risks are increased in patients with a
00233 | personal or family history of substance abuse (including drug or alcohol abuse or addiction) or mental
00234 | illness (e.g., major depression). The potential for these risks should not, however, prevent the proper
00235 | management of pain in any given patient. Patients at increased risk may be prescribed opioids such as
00236 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets, but use in such patients necessitates intensive counseling
00237 | about the risks and proper use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets along with intensive
00238 | monitoring for signs of addiction, abuse, and misuse.
00239 | Opioids are sought by drug abusers and people with addiction disorders and are subject to criminal
00240 | diversion. Consider these risks when prescribing or dispensing acetaminophen and codeine phosphate
00241 | tablets. Strategies to reduce these risks include prescribing the drug in the smallest appropriate quantity
00242 | and advising the patient on the proper disposal of unused drug [see PRECAUTIONS; Information for
00243 | Patients/Caregivers]. Contact local state professional licensing board or state controlled substances
00244 | authority for information on how to prevent and detect abuse or diversion of this product.
00245 | Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression
00246 | Serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression has been reported with the use of opioids,
00247 | even when used as recommended. Respiratory depression, if not immediately recognized and treated,
00248 | may lead to respiratory arrest and death. Management of respiratory depression may include close
00249 | observation, supportive measures, and use of opioid antagonists, depending on the patient’s clinical
00250 | status [see OVERDOSAGE]. Carbon dioxide (CO ) retention from opioid-induced respiratory
00251 | depression can exacerbate the sedating effects of opioids.
00252 | While serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression can occur at any time during the use of
00253 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets, the risk is greatest during the initiation of therapy or
00254 | following a dosage increase. Monitor patients closely for respiratory depression, especially within the
00255 | first 24 to 72 hours of initiating therapy with and following dosage increases of acetaminophen and
00256 | codeine phosphate tablets.
00257 | To reduce the risk of respiratory depression, proper dosing and titration of acetaminophen and codeine
00258 | phosphate tablets are essential [see DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION]. Overestimating the
00259 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets dosage when converting patients from another opioid
00260 | product can result in a fatal overdose with the first dose.
00261 | Accidental ingestion of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets, especially by children, can result
00262 | in respiratory depression and death due to an overdose of codeine.
00263 | Ultra-Rapid Metabolism of Codeine and Other Risk Factors for Life-Threatening Respiratory
00264 | Depression in Children
00265 | Life-threatening respiratory depression and death have occurred in children who received codeine.
00266 | Codeine is subject to variability in metabolism based upon CYP2D6 genotype (described below), which
00267 | can lead to an increased exposure to the active metabolite morphine. Based upon postmarketing reports,
00268 | children younger than 12 years old appear to be more susceptible to the respiratory depressant effects
00269 | of codeine, particularly if there are risk factors for respiratory depression. For example, many reported
00270 | cases of death occurred in the post-operative period following tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy,
00271 | and many of the children had evidence of being ultra-rapid metabolizers of codeine. Furthermore,
00272 | children with obstructive sleep apnea who are treated with codeine for post-tonsillectomy and/or
00273 | adenoidectomy pain may be particularly sensitive to its respiratory depressant effect. Because of the
00274 | risk of life-threatening respiratory depression and death:
00275 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are contraindicated for all children younger than 12
00276 | years of age [see CONTRAINDICATIONS].

00279 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are contraindicated for post-operative management in
00280 | pediatric patients younger than 18 years of age following tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy [see
00281 | CONTRAINDICATIONS].
00282 | Avoid the use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets in adolescents 12 to 18 years of age
00283 | who have other risk factors that may increase their sensitivity to the respiratory depressant effects
00284 | of codeine unless the benefits outweigh the risks. Risk factors include conditions associated with
00285 | hypoventilation, such as postoperative status, obstructive sleep apnea, obesity, severe pulmonary
00286 | disease, neuromuscular disease, and concomitant use of other medications that cause respiratory
00287 | depression [see WARNINGS].
00288 | As with adults, when prescribing codeine for adolescents, healthcare providers should choose the
00289 | lowest effective dose for the shortest period of time and inform patients and caregivers about these
00290 | risks and the signs of morphine overdose [see OVERDOSAGE].
00291 | Nursing Mothers
00292 | At least one death was reported in a nursing infant who was exposed to high levels of morphine in
00293 | breast milk because the mother was an ultra-rapid metabolizer of codeine. Breastfeeding is not
00294 | recommended during treatment with acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets.
00295 | CYP2D6 Genetic Variability: Ultra-Rapid Metabolizers
00296 | Some individuals may be ultra-rapid metabolizers because of a specific CYP2D6 genotype (e.g., gene
00297 | duplications denoted as *1/*1xN or *1/*2xN). The prevalence of this CYP2D6 phenotype varies widely
00298 | and has been estimated at 1 to 10% for Whites (European, North American), 3 to 4% for Blacks (African
00299 | Americans), 1 to 2% for East Asians (Chinese, Japanese, Korean), and may be greater than 10% in
00300 | certain racial/ethnic groups (i.e., Oceanian, Northern African, Middle Eastern, Ashkenazi Jews, Puerto
00301 | Rican).
00302 | These individuals convert codeine into its active metabolite, morphine, more rapidly and completely
00303 | than other people. This rapid conversion results in higher than expected serum morphine levels. Even at
00304 | labeled dosage regimens, individuals who are ultra-rapid metabolizers may have life-threatening or
00305 | fatal respiratory depression or experience signs of overdose (such as extreme sleepiness, confusion,
00306 | or shallow breathing) [see OVERDOSAGE]. Therefore, individuals who are ultra-rapid metabolizers
00307 | should not use acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets.
00308 | Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome
00309 | Prolonged use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets during pregnancy can result in
00310 | withdrawal in the neonate. Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome, unlike opioid withdrawal syndrome in
00311 | adults, may be life-threatening if not recognized and treated, and requires management according to
00312 | protocols developed by neonatology experts. Observe newborns for signs of neonatal opioid
00313 | withdrawal syndrome and manage accordingly. Advise pregnant women using opioids for a prolonged
00314 | period of the risk of neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome and ensure that appropriate treatment will be
00315 | available [see PRECAUTIONS; Information for Patients/Caregivers, Pregnancy].
00316 | Interactions with Drugs Affecting Cytochrome P450 Isoenzymes
00317 | The effects of concomitant use or discontinuation of cytochrome P450 3A4 inducers, 3A4 inhibitors,
00318 | or 2D6 inhibitors with codeine are complex. Use of cytochrome P450 3A4 inducers, 3A4 inhibitors, or
00319 | 2D6 inhibitors with acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets requires careful consideration of the
00320 | effects on the parent drug, codeine, and the active metabolite, morphine.
00321 | Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction
00322 | The concomitant use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets with all cytochrome P450 3A4
00323 | inhibitors, such as macrolide antibiotics (e.g., erythromycin), azole-antifungal agents (e.g.,
00324 | ketoconazole), and protease inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir) or discontinuation of a cytochrome P450 3A4
00325 | inducer such as rifampin, carbamazepine, and phenytoin, may result in an increase in codeine plasma
00326 | 
00327 | concentrations with subsequently greater metabolism by cytochrome P450 2D6, resulting in greater
00328 | morphine levels, which could increase or prolong adverse reactions and may cause potentially fatal
00329 | respiratory depression.
00330 | The concomitant use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets with all cytochrome P450 3A4
00331 | inducers or discontinuation of a cytochrome P450 3A4 inhibitor may result in lower codeine levels,
00332 | greater norcodeine levels, and less metabolism via 2D6 with resultant lower morphine levels. This may
00333 | be associated with a decrease in efficacy, and in some patients, may result in signs and symptoms of
00334 | opioid withdrawal.
00335 | Follow patients receiving acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets and any CYP3A4 inhibitor or
00336 | inducer for signs and symptoms that may reflect opioid toxicity and opioid withdrawal when
00337 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are used in conjunction with inhibitors and inducers of
00338 | CYP3A4 [see WARNINGS, PRECAUTIONS; Drug Interactions].
00339 | If concomitant use of a CYP3A4 inhibitor is necessary or if a CYP3A4 inducer is discontinued,
00340 | consider dosage reduction of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets until stable drug effects are
00341 | achieved. Monitor patients for respiratory depression and sedation at frequent intervals.
00342 | If concomitant use of a CYP3A4 inducer is necessary or if a CYP3A4 inhibitor is discontinued,
00343 | consider increasing the acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets dosage until stable drug effects
00344 | are achieved. Monitor for signs of opioid withdrawal [see PRECAUTIONS; Drug Interactions].
00345 | Risks of Concomitant Use or Discontinuation of Cytochrome P450 2D6 Inhibitors
00346 | The concomitant use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets with all cytochrome P450 2D6
00347 | inhibitors (e.g., amiodarone, quinidine) may result in an increase in codeine plasma concentrations and a
00348 | decrease in active metabolite morphine plasma concentration which could result in an analgesic efficacy
00349 | reduction or symptoms of opioid withdrawal.
00350 | Discontinuation of a concomitantly used cytochrome P450 2D6 inhibitor may result in a decrease in
00351 | codeine plasma concentration and an increase in active metabolite morphine plasma concentration which
00352 | could increase or prolong adverse reactions and may cause potentially fatal respiratory depression.
00353 | Follow patients receiving acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets and any CYP2D6 inhibitor for
00354 | signs and symptoms that may reflect opioid toxicity and opioid withdrawal when acetaminophen and
00355 | codeine phosphate tablets are used in conjunction with inhibitors of CYP2D6.
00356 | If concomitant use with a CYP2D6 inhibitor is necessary, follow the patient for signs of reduced
00357 | efficacy or opioid withdrawal and consider increasing the acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets
00358 | dosage. After stopping use of a CYP2D6 inhibitor, consider reducing the acetaminophen and codeine
00359 | phosphate tablets dosage and follow the patient for signs and symptoms of respiratory depression or
00360 | sedation [see PRECAUTIONS; Drug Interactions].
00361 | Hepatotoxicity
00362 | Acetaminophen has been associated with cases of acute liver failure, at times resulting in liver
00363 | transplant and death. Most of the cases of liver injury are associated with the use of acetaminophen at
00364 | doses that exceed 4,000 milligrams per day, and often involve more than one acetaminophen-containing
00365 | product. The excessive intake of acetaminophen may be intentional to cause self-harm or unintentional
00366 | as patients attempt to obtain more pain relief or unknowingly take other acetaminophen-containing
00367 | products.
00368 | The risk of acute liver failure is higher in individuals with underlying liver disease and in individuals
00369 | who ingest alcohol while taking acetaminophen.
00370 | Instruct patients to look for acetaminophen or APAP on package labels and not to use more than one
00371 | product that contains acetaminophen. Instruct patients to seek medical attention immediately upon
00372 | ingestion of more than 4,000 milligrams of acetaminophen per day, even if they feel well.
00373 | Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants
00374 | 
00375 | Profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death may result from the concomitant use of
00376 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets with benzodiazepines and/or other CNS depressants (e.g.,
00377 | non-benzodiazepine sedatives/hypnotics, anxiolytics, tranquilizers, muscle relaxants, general
00378 | anesthetics, antipsychotics, other opioids, alcohol). Because of these risks, reserve concomitant
00379 | prescribing of these drugs for use in patients for whom alternative treatment options are inadequate.
00380 | Observational studies have demonstrated that concomitant use of opioid analgesics and benzodiazepines
00381 | increases the risk of drug-related mortality compared to use of opioid analgesics alone. Because of
00382 | similar pharmacological properties, it is reasonable to expect similar risk with the concomitant use of
00383 | other CNS depressant drugs with opioid analgesics [see PRECAUTIONS; Drug Interactions].
00384 | If the decision is made to prescribe a benzodiazepine or other CNS depressant concomitantly with an
00385 | opioid analgesic, prescribe the lowest effective dosages and minimum durations of concomitant use. In
00386 | patients already receiving an opioid analgesic, prescribe a lower initial dose of the benzodiazepine or
00387 | other CNS depressant than indicated in the absence of an opioid, and titrate based on clinical response.
00388 | If an opioid analgesic is initiated in a patient already taking a benzodiazepine or other CNS depressant,
00389 | prescribe a lower initial dose of the opioid analgesic, and titrate based on clinical response. Follow
00390 | patients closely for signs and symptoms of respiratory depression and sedation.
00391 | Advise both patients and caregivers about the risks of respiratory depression and sedation when
00392 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are used with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants
00393 | (including alcohol and illicit drugs). Advise patients not to drive or operate heavy machinery until the
00394 | effects of concomitant use of the benzodiazepine or other CNS depressant have been determined.
00395 | Screen patients for risk of substance use disorders, including opioid abuse and misuse, and warn them
00396 | of the risk for overdose and death associated with the use of additional CNS depressants including
00397 | alcohol and illicit drugs [see PRECAUTIONS; Drug Interactions, Information for

00424 | Cases of adrenal insufficiency have been reported with opioid use, more often following greater than 1

00595 | Drug Interactions
00596 | CYP2D6 Inhibitors
00597 | Codeine is metabolized by CYP2D6 to form morphine. The concomitant use of acetaminophen and
00598 | codeine phosphate tablets and CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion, quinidine)
00599 | can increase the plasma concentration of codeine, but can decrease the plasma concentration of active
00600 | metabolite morphine, which could result in reduced analgesic efficacy or symptoms of opioid
00601 | withdrawal, particularly when an inhibitor is added after a stable dose of acetaminophen and codeine
00602 | phosphate tablets are achieved [see CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY].
00603 | After stopping a CYP2D6 inhibitor, as the effects of the inhibitor decline, the codeine plasma
00604 | concentration will decrease but the active metabolite morphine plasma concentration will increase,
00605 | which could increase or prolong adverse reactions and may cause potentially fatal respiratory
00606 | depression [see CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY].
00607 | 
00608 | If concomitant use with a CYP2D6 inhibitor is necessary, or if a CYP2D6 inhibitor is discontinued after
00609 | concomitant use, consider dosage adjustment of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets and
00610 | monitor patients closely at frequent intervals.
00611 | If concomitant use with CYP2D6 inhibitors is necessary, follow the patient for reduced efficacy or
00612 | signs and symptoms of opioid withdrawal and consider increasing the acetaminophen and codeine
00613 | phosphate tablets as needed.
00614 | After stopping use of a CYP2D6 inhibitor, consider reducing the acetaminophen and codeine phosphate
00615 | tablets and monitor the patient for signs and symptoms of respiratory depression or sedation.
00616 | CYP3A4 Inhibitors
00617 | The concomitant use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets and CYP3A4 inhibitors, such as
00618 | macrolide antibiotics (e.g., erythromycin), azole-antifungal agents (e.g. ketoconazole), and protease
00619 | inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir), may result in an increase in codeine plasma concentrations, with subsequently
00620 | greater metabolism by cytochrome CYP2D6, resulting in greater morphine levels, which could increase
00621 | or prolong adverse reactions and may cause potentially fatal respiratory depression, particularly when
00622 | an inhibitor is added after a stable dose of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets is achieved
00623 | [see WARNINGS].
00624 | After stopping a CYP3A4 inhibitor, as the effects of the inhibitor decline, it may result in lower
00625 | codeine levels, greater norcodeine levels, and less metabolism via CYP2D6 with resultant lower
00626 | morphine levels [see CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY], resulting in decreased opioid efficacy or a
00627 | withdrawal syndrome in patients who had developed physical dependence to codeine.
00628 | If concomitant use of CYP3A4 inhibitor is necessary, consider dosage reduction of acetaminophen and
00629 | codeine tablets until stable drug effects are achieved. Monitor patients for respiratory depression and
00630 | sedation at frequent intervals.
00631 | If a CYP3A4 inhibitor is discontinued, consider increasing the acetaminophen and codeine phosphate
00632 | tablets dosage until stable drug effects are achieved. Monitor for signs of opioid withdrawal.
00633 | CYP3A4 Inducers
00634 | The concomitant use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets and CYP3A4 inducers (e.g.,
00635 | rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) can result in lower codeine levels, greater norcodeine levels, and
00636 | less metabolism via 2D6 with resultant lower morphine levels [see CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY],
00637 | resulting in decreased efficacy or onset of a withdrawal syndrome in patients who have developed
00638 | physical dependence [see WARNINGS].
00639 | After stopping a CYP3A4 inducer, as the effects of the inducer decline, codeine plasma concentrations
00640 | may increase, with subsequently greater metabolism by cytochrome CYP2D6, resulting in greater
00641 | morphine levels [see CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY], which could increase or prolong both the
00642 | therapeutic effects and adverse reactions, and may cause serious respiratory depression.

00644 | of opioid withdrawal and consider increasing the acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets dosage
00645 | as needed.
00646 | If a CYP3A4 inducer is discontinued, consider acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets dosage

00703 | Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
00704 | Carcinogenesis
00705 | Long-term studies to evaluate the carcinogenic potential of the combination of codeine and
00706 | acetaminophen have not been conducted.
00707 | Two-year carcinogenicity studies have been conducted in F344/N rats and B6C3F1 mice. There was no
00708 | evidence of carcinogenicity in male and female rats, respectively, at dietary doses up to 70 and 80
00709 | mg/kg/day of codeine sulfate (approximately 2 times the maximum recommended daily dose of 360
00710 | mg/day for adults on a mg/m basis) for two years. Similarly there was no evidence of carcinogenicity
00711 | activity in male and female mice at dietary doses up to 400 mg/kg/day of codeine sulfate (approximately
00712 | 5 times the maximum recommended daily dose of 360 mg/day for adults on a mg/m basis) for two
00713 | years.
00714 | Long-term studies in mice and rats have been completed by the National Toxicology Program to
00715 | evaluate the carcinogenic potential of acetaminophen. In 2-year feeding studies, F344/N rats and
00716 | B6C3F1 mice were fed a diet containing acetaminophen up to 6000 ppm. Female rats demonstrated
00717 | equivocal evidence of carcinogenic activity based on increased incidences of mononuclear cell
00718 | leukemia at 0.8 times the maximum human daily dose (MHDD) of 4 grams/day, based on a body surface
00719 | area comparison. In contrast, there was no evidence of carcinogenic activity in male rats that received
00720 | up to 0.7 times or mice at up to 1.2 to 1.4 times the MHDD, based on a body surface area comparison.
00721 | Mutagenesis
00722 | Codeine sulfate was not mutagenic in the in vitro bacterial reverse mutation assay or clastogenic in the in
00723 | vitro Chinese hamster ovary cell chromosome aberration assay.
00724 | In the published literature, acetaminophen has been reported to be clastogenic when administered at
00725 | 1500 mg/kg/day to the rat model (3.6-times the MHDD, based on a body surface area comparison). In
00726 | contrast, no clastogenicity was noted at a dose of 750 mg/kg/day (1.8-times the MHDD, based on a body
00727 | surface area comparison), suggesting a threshold effect.
00728 | Impairment of Fertility
00729 | No nonclinical fertility studies have been conducted with codeine or the combination of codeine and
00730 | acetaminophen.
00731 | In studies conducted by the National Toxicology Program, fertility assessments with acetaminophen
00732 | have been completed in Swiss CD-1 mice via a continuous breeding study. There were no effects on
00733 | fertility parameters in mice consuming up to 1.7 times the MHDD of acetaminophen, based on a body
00734 | surface area comparison.
00735 | Although there was no effect on sperm motility or sperm density in the epididymis, there was a
00736 | significant increase in the percentage of abnormal sperm in mice consuming 1.78 times the MHDD
00737 | (based on a body surface comparison) and there was a reduction in the number of mating pairs producing
00738 | a fifth litter at this dose, suggesting the potential for cumulative toxicity with chronic administration of
00739 | acetaminophen near the upper limit of daily dosing.
00740 | Published studies in rodents report that oral acetaminophen treatment of male animals at doses that are
00741 | 1.2 times the MHDD and greater (based on a body surface comparison) result in decreased testicular
00742 | weights, reduced spermatogenesis, reduced fertility, and reduced implantation sites in females given the
00743 | same doses. These effects appear to increase with the duration of treatment. The clinical significance
00744 | of these findings is not known.

00762 | Nonteratogenic Effects
00763 | Fetal/Neonatal Adverse Reactions
00764 | Prolonged use of opioid analgesics during pregnancy for medical or nonmedical purposes can result in
00765 | physical dependence in the neonate and neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome shortly after birth.
00766 | Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome presents as irritability, hyperactivity and abnormal sleep pattern,
00767 | high pitched cry, tremor, vomiting, diarrhea and failure to gain weight. The onset, duration, and severity
00768 | of neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome vary based on the specific opioid used, duration of use, timing
00769 | and amount of last maternal use, and rate of elimination of the drug by the newborn. Observe newborns
00770 | for symptoms of neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome and manage accordingly [see WARNINGS].
00771 | Labor or Delivery
00772 | Opioids cross the placenta and may produce respiratory depression and psycho-physiologic effects in
00773 | neonates. An opioid antagonist, such as naloxone, must be available for reversal of opioid-induced
00774 | respiratory depression in the neonate. Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are not
00775 | recommended for use in pregnant women during or immediately prior to labor, when other analgesic
00776 | techniques are more appropriate. Opioid analgesics, including acetaminophen and codeine phosphate
00777 | tablets, and can prolong labor through actions which temporarily reduce the strength, duration, and
00778 | frequency of uterine contractions. However, this effect is not consistent and may be offset by an
00779 | increased rate of cervical dilation, which tends to shorten labor. Monitor neonates exposed to opioid
00780 | analgesics during labor for signs of excess sedation and respiratory depression.
00781 | Narcotic analgesics should be avoided during labor if delivery of a premature infant is anticipated. If
00782 | the mother has received narcotic analgesics during labor, newborn infants should be observed closely
00783 | for signs of respiratory depression. Resuscitation may be required [see OVERDOSAGE]. The effect
00784 | of codeine, if any, on the later growth, development, and functional maturation of the child is unknown.
00785 | Nursing Mothers
00786 | Codeine and its active metabolite, morphine, are present in human milk. There are published studies and
00787 | cases that have reported excessive sedation, respiratory depression, and death in infants exposed to
00788 | codeine via breast milk. Women who are ultra-rapid metabolizers of codeine achieve higher than
00789 | expected serum levels of morphine, potentially leading to higher levels of morphine in breast milk that
00790 | can be dangerous in their breastfed infants. In women with normal codeine metabolism (normal CYP2D6
00791 | activity), the amount of codeine secreted into human milk is low and dose-dependent.
00792 | There is no information on the effects of codeine on milk production. Because of the potential for
00793 | serious adverse reactions, including excess sedation, respiratory depression, and death in a breastfed
00794 | infant, advise patients that breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment with acetaminophen and
00795 | 
00796 | codeine phosphate tablets [see WARNINGS].
00797 | Acetaminophen is excreted in breast milk in small amounts, but the significance of its effect on nursing
00798 | infants is not known. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from
00799 | acetaminophen, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug,
00800 | taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.

00804 | breastfed infants when maternal administration of an opioid analgesic is stopped, or when breastfeeding
00805 | is stopped.
00806 | Pediatric Use
00807 | The safety and effectiveness of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets in pediatric patients below
00808 | the age of 18 have not been established.
00809 | Life-threatening respiratory depression and death have occurred in children who received codeine [see
00810 | WARNINGS]. In most of the reported cases, these events followed tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy,
00811 | and many of the children had evidence of being ultra-rapid metabolizers of codeine (i.e., multiple
00812 | copies of the gene for cytochrome P450 isoenzyme 2D6 or high morphine concentrations). Children
00813 | with sleep apnea may be particularly sensitive to the respiratory depressant effects of codeine. Because
00814 | of the risk of life-threatening respiratory depression and death:

00816 | years of age [see CONTRAINDICATIONS].
00817 | Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are contraindicated for post-operative management in
00818 | pediatric patients younger than 18 years of age following tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy [see
00819 | CONTRAINDICATIONS].
00820 | Avoid the use of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets in adolescents 12 to 18 years of age
00821 | who have other risk factors that may increase their sensitivity to the respiratory depressant effects
00822 | of codeine unless the benefits outweigh the risks. Risk factors include conditions associated with
00823 | hypoventilation, such as postoperative status, obstructive sleep apnea, obesity, severe pulmonary
00824 | disease, neuromuscular disease, and concomitant use of other medications that cause respiratory
00825 | depression [see WARNINGS].
00826 | Geriatric Use
00827 | Elderly patients (aged 65 years or older) may have increased sensitivity to acetaminophen and codeine
00828 | phosphate tablets. In general, use caution when selecting a dosage for an elderly patient, usually starting
00829 | at the low end of the dosing range, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or
00830 | cardiac function and of concomitant disease or other drug therapy.
00831 | Respiratory depression is the chief risk for elderly patients treated with opioids, and has occurred after
00832 | large initial doses were administered to patients who were not opioid-tolerant or when opioids were
00833 | coadministered with other agents that depress respiration. Titrate the dosage of acetaminophen and
00834 | codeine phosphate tablets slowly in geriatric patients and monitor closely for signs of central nervous
00835 | system depression [see WARNINGS].
00836 | These drugs are known to be substantially excreted by the kidney, and the risk of adverse reactions to
00837 | this drug may be greater in patients with impaired renal function. Because elderly patients are more
00838 | likely to have decreased renal function, care should be taken in dose selection, and it may be useful to
00839 | monitor renal function.

00898 | All patients treated with opioids require careful monitoring for signs of abuse and addiction, because
00899 | use of opioid analgesic products carries the risk of addiction even under appropriate medical use.
00900 | Prescription drug abuse is the intentional non-therapeutic use of a prescription drug, even once, for its
00901 | rewarding psychological or physiological effects.
00902 | Drug addiction is a cluster of behavioral, cognitive, and physiological phenomena that develop after
00903 | repeated substance use and includes: a strong desire to take the drug, difficulties in controlling its use,
00904 | persisting in its use despite harmful, or potentially harmful, consequences, a higher priority given to
00905 | drug use than to other activities and obligations, increased tolerance, and sometimes a physical
00906 | withdrawal.
00907 | “Drug-seeking” behavior is very common in persons with substance use disorders. Drug-seeking
00908 | tactics include emergency calls or visits near the end of office hours, refusal to undergo appropriate
00909 | examination, testing, or referral, repeated “loss” of prescriptions, tampering with prescriptions and
00910 | reluctance to provide prior medical records or contact information for other treating health care
00911 | providers. “Doctor shopping” (visiting multiple prescribers to obtain additional prescriptions) is
00912 | common among drug abusers and people suffering from untreated addiction. Preoccupation with
00913 | achieving adequate pain relief can be appropriate behavior in a patient with poor pain control.
00914 | Abuse and addiction are separate and distinct from physical dependence and tolerance. Healthcare
00915 | providers should be aware that addiction may not be accompanied by concurrent tolerance and symptoms
00916 | of physical dependence in all addicts. In addition, abuse of opioids can occur in the absence of true
00917 | addiction.
00918 | Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets, like other opioids, can be diverted for non-medical use
00919 | into illicit channels of distribution. Careful record-keeping of prescribing information, including
00920 | quantity, frequency, and renewal requests, as required by state and federal law, is strongly advised.
00921 | Proper assessment of the patient, proper prescribing practices, periodic re-evaluation of therapy, and
00922 | proper dispensing and storage are appropriate measures that help to limit abuse of opioid drugs.
00923 | Risks Specific to Abuse of Acetaminophen and Codeine Phosphate Tablets
00924 | Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are for oral use only. Abuse of acetaminophen and
00925 | codeine phosphate tablets poses a risk of overdose and death. The risk is increased with concurrent use
00926 | of acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets with alcohol and other central nervous system
00927 | depressants.
00928 | Parenteral drug abuse is commonly associated with transmission of infectious diseases such as hepatitis
00929 | and HIV.
00930 | Dependence
00931 | Both tolerance and physical dependence can develop during chronic opioid therapy. Tolerance is the
00932 | need for increasing doses of opioids to maintain a defined effect such as analgesia (in the absence of
00933 | disease progression or other external factors). Tolerance may occur to both the desired and undesired
00934 | effects of drugs, and may develop at different rates for different effects.
00935 | Physical dependence results in withdrawal symptoms after abrupt discontinuation or a significant dosage
00936 | reduction of a drug. Withdrawal also may be precipitated through the administration of drugs with
00937 | opioid antagonist activity (e.g., naloxone, nalmefene), mixed agonist/antagonist analgesics (e.g.,
00938 | pentazocine, butorphanol, nalbuphine), or partial agonists (e.g., buprenorphine). Physical dependence
00939 | may not occur to a clinically significant degree until after several days to weeks of continued opioid
00940 | usage.
00941 | Acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets should not be abruptly discontinued [see DOSAGE AND
00942 | ADMINISTRATION]. If acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets are abruptly discontinued in a
00943 | 
00944 | physically dependent patient, a withdrawal syndrome may occur. Some or all of the following can
00945 | characterize this syndrome: restlessness, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, yawning, perspiration, chills, myalgia,
00946 | and mydriasis. Other signs and symptoms also may develop, including: irritability, anxiety, backache,
00947 | joint pain, weakness, abdominal cramps, insomnia, nausea, anorexia, vomiting, diarrhea, or increased
00948 | blood pressure, respiratory rate, or heart rate.
00949 | Infants born to mothers physically dependent on opioids will also be physically dependent and may
00950 | exhibit respiratory difficulties and withdrawal signs [see PRECAUTIONS; Pregnancy].
00951 | OVERDOSAGE
00952 | Following an acute overdosage, toxicity may result from codeine or acetaminophen.
00953 | Clinical Presentation
00954 | Codeine
00955 | Acute overdosage with codeine can be manifested by respiratory depression, somnolence progressing
00956 | to stupor or coma, skeletal muscle flaccidity, cold and clammy skin, constricted pupils, and, in some
00957 | cases, pulmonary edema, bradycardia, hypotension, partial or complete airway obstruction, atypical
00958 | snoring, and death. Marked mydriasis rather than miosis may be seen with hypoxia in overdose
00959 | situations.
00960 | Acetaminophen
00961 | Dose-dependent, potentially fatal hepatic necrosis is the most serious adverse effect of acetaminophen
00962 | overdose. Renal tubular necrosis, hypoglycemic coma, and coagulation defects may also occur.
00963 | Early symptoms following a potentially hepatotoxic overdose may include; anorexia, nausea, vomiting,
00964 | diaphoresis, pallor and general malaise. Clinical and laboratory evidence of hepatic toxicity may not be
00965 | apparent until 48 to 72 hours post-ingestion.
00966 | Treatment of Overdose
00967 | Codeine
00968 | In case of overdose, priorities are the reestablishment of a patent and protected airway and institution of
00969 | assisted or controlled ventilation, if needed. Employ other supportive measures (including oxygen and
00970 | vasopressors) in the management of circulatory shock and pulmonary edema as indicated. Cardiac arrest
00971 | or serious arrhythmias will require advanced life-support measures.
00972 | The opioid antagonists, naloxone or nalmefene, are specific antidotes to respiratory depression
00973 | resulting from opioid overdose. For clinically significant respiratory or circulatory depression
00974 | secondary to acetaminophen and codeine overdose, administer an opioid antagonist. Opioid antagonists
00975 | should not be administered in the absence of clinically significant respiratory or circulatory depression
00976 | secondary to codeine overdose.
00977 | Because the duration of opioid reversal is expected to be less than the duration of action of codeine in
00978 | acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets, carefully monitor the patient until spontaneous respiration
00979 | is reliably reestablished. If the response to an opioid antagonist is suboptimal or only brief in nature,
00980 | administer additional antagonist as directed by the product’s prescribing information.
00981 | In an individual physically dependent on opioids, administration of the recommended usual dosage of the
00982 | antagonist will precipitate an acute withdrawal syndrome. The severity of the withdrawal symptoms
00983 | experienced will depend on the degree of physical dependence and the dose of the antagonist
00984 | administered. If a decision is made to treat serious respiratory depression in the physically dependent
00985 | patient, administration of the antagonist should be begun with care and by titration with smaller than usual
00986 | doses of the antagonist.

00998 | absorption of the drug must be readily performed since the hepatic injury is dose-dependent and occurs
00999 | early in the course of intoxication.
01000 | DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
01001 | Important Dosage and Administration Instructions
01002 | Use the lowest effective dosage for the shortest duration consistent with individual patient treatment
01003 | goals [see WARNINGS].
01004 | Initiate the dosing regimen for each patient individually, taking into account the patient's severity of pain,
01005 | patient response, prior analgesic treatment experience, and risk factors for addiction, abuse, and misuse
01006 | [see WARNINGS].
01007 | Monitor patients closely for respiratory depression, especially within the first 24 to 72 hours of
01008 | initiating therapy and following dosage increases with acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets and
01009 | adjust the dosage accordingly [see WARNINGS].
01010 | Initial Dosage
01011 | Initiating Treatment with Acetaminophen and Codeine Phosphate Tablets
01012 | Dosage should be adjusted according to severity of pain and response of the patient. However, it
01013 | should be kept in mind that tolerance to codeine can develop with continued use and that the incidence of
01014 | untoward effects is dose related. Adult doses of codeine higher than 60 mg are associated with an
01015 | increased incidence of adverse reactions and are not associated with greater efficacy.
01016 | The usual adult dosage is:
01017 | Acetaminophen and Codeine Phosphate Tablets (codeine 30 mg and acetaminophen 300 mg): Take 1
01018 | to 2 tablets every 4 hours as needed for pain.
01019 | Acetaminophen and Codeine Phosphate Tablets (codeine 60 mg and acetaminophen 300 mg): Take
01020 | one tablet every 4 hours as needed for pain.

01034 | Conversion from Other Opioids to Acetaminophen and Codeine Phosphate Tablets
01035 | There is inter-patient variability in the potency of opioid drugs and opioid formulations. Therefore, a
01036 | conservative approach is advised when determining the total daily dosage of acetaminophen and codeine

01039 | tablets dosage than to overestimate the 24-hour acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets dosage
01040 | and manage an adverse reaction due to overdose.
01041 | Titration and Maintenance of Therapy
01042 | Individually titrate acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets to a dose that provides adequate
01043 | analgesia and minimizes adverse reactions. Continually reevaluate patients receiving acetaminophen and
01044 | codeine phosphate tablets to assess the maintenance of pain control and the relative incidence of
01045 | adverse reactions, as well as monitoring for the development of addiction, abuse, or misuse [see
01046 | WARNINGS]. Frequent communication is important among the prescriber, other members of the
01047 | healthcare team, the patient, and the caregiver/family during periods of changing analgesic
01048 | requirements, including initial titration.
01049 | If the level of pain increases after dosage stabilization, attempt to identify the source of increased pain
01050 | before increasing the acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets dosage. If unacceptable opioid-
01051 | related adverse reactions are observed, consider reducing the dosage. Adjust the dosage to obtain an
01052 | appropriate balance between management of pain and opioid-related adverse reactions.
01053 | Discontinuation of Acetaminophen and Codeine Phosphate Tablets
01054 | When a patient who has been taking acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets regularly and may be
01055 | physically dependent no longer requires therapy with acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets,
01056 | taper the dose gradually, by 25% to 50% every 2 to 4 days, while monitoring carefully for signs and
01057 | symptoms of withdrawal. If the patient develops these signs or symptoms, raise the dose to the previous
01058 | level and taper more slowly, either by increasing the interval between decreases, decreasing the amount
01059 | of change in dose, or both. Do not abruptly discontinue acetaminophen and codeine phosphate tablets in
01060 | a physically-dependent patient [see WARNINGS, DRUG ABUSE AND DEPENDENCE].
