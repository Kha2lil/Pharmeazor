00007 | Methyldopa is an antihypertensive drug.
00008 | Methyldopa is the L-isomer of alpha-methyldopa. Its chemical name is levo-3-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)-
00009 | 2-methylalanine sesquihydrate. Its structural formula is:

00014 | Each tablet, for oral administration, contains 250 mg or 500 mg of methyldopa. Potency is calculated in

00019 | CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
00020 | Methyldopa is an aromatic-amino-acid decarboxylase inhibitor in animals and in man. Although the
00021 | mechanism of action has yet to be conclusively demonstrated, the antihypertensive effect of methyldopa
00022 | probably is due to its metabolism to alpha-methylnorepinephrine, which then lowers arterial pressure by
00023 | stimulation of central inhibitory alpha-adrenergic receptors, false neurotransmission, and/or reduction
00024 | of plasma renin activity. Methyldopa has been shown to cause a net reduction in the tissue concentration
00025 | of serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine.
00026 | Only methyldopa, the L-isomer of alpha-methyldopa, has the ability to inhibit dopa decarboxylase and to
00027 | deplete animal tissues of norepinephrine. In man, the antihypertensive activity appears to be due solely
00028 | to the L-isomer. About twice the dose of the racemate (DL-alpha-methyldopa) is required for equal
00029 | antihypertensive effect.
00030 | Methyldopa has no direct effect on cardiac function and usually does not reduce glomerular filtration
00031 | rate, renal blood flow, or filtration fraction. Cardiac output usually is maintained without cardiac
00032 | acceleration. In some patients the heart rate is slowed.
00033 | Normal or elevated plasma renin activity may decrease in the course of methyldopa therapy.
00034 | Methyldopa reduces both supine and standing blood pressure. Methyldopa usually produces highly

00041 | hypotension and diurnal blood pressure variations rarely occur.
00042 | Pharmacokinetics and Metabolism
00043 | The maximum decrease in blood pressure occurs four to six hours after oral dosage. Once an effective
00044 | dosage level is attained, a smooth blood pressure response occurs in most patients in 12 to 24 hours.
00045 | After withdrawal, blood pressure usually returns to pretreatment levels within 24-48 hours.
00046 | Methyldopa is extensively metabolized. The known urinary metabolites are: α-methyldopa mono-0-
00047 | sulfate; 3-0-methyl-α-methyldopa; 3,4,-dihydroxyphenylacetone; α-methyldopamine; 3-0-methyl-α-
00048 | methyldopamine and their conjugates.
00049 | Approximately 70% of the drug which is absorbed is excreted in the urine as methyldopa and its mono-
00050 | 0-sulfate conjugate. The renal clearance is about 130 mL/min. in normal subjects and is diminished in
00051 | renal insufficiency. The plasma half-life of methyldopa is 105 minutes. After oral doses, excretion is
00052 | essentially complete in 36 hours.
00053 | Methyldopa crosses the placental barrier, appears in cord blood, and appears in breast milk.
00054 | INDICATIONS AND USAGE
00055 | Hypertension.
00056 | CONTRAINDICATIONS
00057 | Methyldopa is contraindicated in patients:
00058 | - with active hepatic disease, such as acute hepatitis and active cirrhosis.
00059 | - with liver disorders previously associated with methyldopa therapy (see WARNINGS).
00060 | - with hypersensitivity to any component of this product.
00061 | - on therapy with monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors.
00062 | WARNINGS
00063 | It is important to recognize that a positive Coombs test, hemolytic anemia, and liver disorders
00064 | may occur with methyldopa therapy. The rare occurrences of hemolytic anemia or liver
00065 | disorders could lead to potentially fatal complications unless properly recognized and managed.
00066 | Read this section carefully to understand these reactions.
00067 | With prolonged methyldopa therapy, 10 to 20 percent of patients develop a positive direct Coombs test
00068 | which usually occurs between 6 and 12 months of methyldopa therapy. Lowest incidence is at daily
00069 | dosage of 1 g or less. This on rare occasions may be associated with hemolytic anemia, which could
00070 | lead to potentially fatal complications. One cannot predict which patients with a positive direct Coombs
00071 | test may develop hemolytic anemia.
00072 | Prior existence or development of a positive direct Coombs test is not in itself a contraindication to use
00073 | of methyldopa. If a positive Coombs test develops during methyldopa therapy, the physician should
00074 | determine whether hemolytic anemia exists and whether the positive Coombs test may be a problem. For
00075 | example, in addition to a positive direct Coombs test there is less often a positive indirect Coombs test
00076 | which may interfere with cross matching of blood.
00077 | Before treatment is started, it is desirable to do a blood count (hematocrit, hemoglobin, or red cell
00078 | count) for a baseline or to establish whether there is anemia. Periodic blood counts should be done
00079 | during therapy to detect hemolytic anemia. It may be useful to do a direct Coombs test before therapy
00080 | and at 6 and 12 months after the start of therapy.
00081 | 
00082 | If Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia occurs, the cause may be methyldopa and the drug should be
00083 | discontinued. Usually the anemia remits promptly. If not, corticosteroids may be given and other causes
00084 | of anemia should be considered. If the hemolytic anemia is related to methyldopa, the drug should not
00085 | be reinstituted.
00086 | When methyldopa causes Coombs positivity alone or with hemolytic anemia, the red cell is usually
00087 | coated with gamma globulin of the IgG (gamma G) class only. The positive Coombs test may not revert
00088 | to normal until weeks to months after methyldopa is stopped.
00089 | Should the need for transfusion arise in a patient receiving methyldopa, both a direct and an indirect
00090 | Coombs test should be performed. In the absence of hemolytic anemia, usually only the direct Coombs
00091 | test will be positive. A positive direct Coombs test alone will not interfere with typing or cross
00092 | matching. If the indirect Coombs test is also positive, problems may arise in the major cross match and
00093 | the assistance of a hematologist or transfusion expert will be needed.
00094 | Occasionally, fever has occurred within the first 3 weeks of methyldopa therapy, associated in some
00095 | cases with eosinophilia or abnormalities in one or more liver function tests, such as serum alkaline
00096 | phosphatase, serum transaminases (SGOT, SGPT), bilirubin and prothrombin time. Jaundice, with or
00097 | without fever, may occur with onset, usually within the first 2 or 3 months of therapy. In some patients
00098 | the findings are consistent with those of cholestasis. In others the findings are consistent with hepatitis
00099 | and hepatocellular injury.
00100 | Rarely, fatal hepatic necrosis has been reported after use of methyldopa. These hepatic changes may
00101 | represent hypersensitivity reactions. Periodic determinations of hepatic function should be done,
00102 | particularly during the first 6 to 12 weeks of therapy or whenever an unexplained fever occurs. If fever,
00103 | abnormalities in liver function tests, or jaundice appear, stop therapy with methyldopa. If caused by
00104 | methyldopa, the temperature and abnormalities in liver function characteristically have reverted to
00105 | normal when the drug was discontinued. Methyldopa should not be reinstituted in such patients.
00106 | Rarely, a reversible reduction of the white blood cell count with a primary effect on the granulocytes
00107 | has been seen. The granulocyte count returned promptly to normal on discontinuance of the drug. Rare
00108 | cases of granulocytopenia have been reported. In each instance, upon stopping the drug, the white cell
00109 | count returned to normal. Reversible thrombocytopenia has occurred rarely.
00110 | PRECAUTIONS
00111 | General
00112 | Methyldopa should be used with caution in patients with a history of previous liver disease or
00113 | dysfunction (See WARNINGS).
00114 | Some patients taking methyldopa experience clinical edema or weight gain which may be controlled by
00115 | use of a diuretic. Methyldopa should not be continued if edema progresses or signs of heart failure
00116 | appear.
00117 | Hypertension has recurred occasionally after dialysis in patients given methyldopa because the drug is
00118 | removed by this procedure.
00119 | Rarely involuntary choreoathetotic movements have been observed during therapy with methyldopa in
00120 | patients with severe bilateral cerebrovascular disease. Should these movements occur, stop therapy.
00121 | Laboratory Tests
00122 | Blood count, Coombs tests, and liver function tests are recommended before initiating therapy and at
00123 | periodic intervals (See WARNINGS).
00124 | Drug Interactions
00125 | When methyldopa is used with other antihypertensive drugs, potentiation of antihypertensive effect may
00126 | 
00127 | occur. Patients should be followed carefully to detect side reactions or unusual manifestations of drug
00128 | idiosyncrasy.
00129 | Patients may require reduced doses of anesthetics when on methyldopa. If hypotension does occur
00130 | during anesthesia, it usually can be controlled by vasopressors. The adrenergic receptors remain
00131 | sensitive during treatment with methyldopa.
00132 | When methyldopa and lithium are given concomitantly the patient should be carefully monitored for
00133 | symptoms of lithium toxicity. Read the circular for lithium preparations.
00134 | Several studies demonstrate a decrease in the bioavailability of methyldopa when it is ingested with
00135 | ferrous sulfate or ferrous gluconate. This may adversely affect blood pressure control in patients
00136 | treated with methyldopa. Coadministration of methyldopa with ferrous sulfate or ferrous gluconate is
00137 | not recommended.
00138 | Monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors: see CONTRAINDICATIONS.
00139 | Drug/Laboratory Test Interactions
00140 | Methyldopa may interfere with measurement of: urinary uric acid by the phosphotungstate method,
00141 | serum creatinine by the alkaline picrate method, and SGOT by colorimetric methods. Interference with
00142 | spectrophotometric methods for SGOT analysis has not been reported.
00143 | Since methyldopa causes fluorescence in urine samples at the same wavelengths as catecholamines,
00144 | falsely high levels of urinary catecholamines may be reported. This will interfere with the diagnosis of
00145 | pheochromocytoma. It is important to recognize this phenomenon before a patient with a possible
00146 | pheochromocytoma is subjected to surgery. Methyldopa does not interfere with measurement of VMA
00147 | (vanillylmandelic acid), a test for pheochromocytoma, by those methods which convert VMA to vanillin.
00148 | Methyldopa is not recommended for the treatment of patients with pheochromocytoma. Rarely, when
00149 | urine is exposed to air after voiding, it may darken because of breakdown of methyldopa or its
00150 | metabolites.
00151 | Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
00152 | No evidence of a tumorigenic effect was seen when methyldopa was given for two years to mice at
00153 | doses up to 1800 mg/kg/day or to rats at doses up to 240 mg/kg/day. (30 and 4 times the maximum
00154 | recommended human dose in mice and rats, respectively, when compared on the basis of body weight;
00155 | 2.5 and 0.6 times the maximum recommended human dose in mice and rats, respectively, when compared
00156 | on the basis of body surface area; calculations assume a patient weight of 50 kg).
00157 | Methyldopa was not mutagenic in the Ames Test and did not increase chromosomal aberration or sister
00158 | chromatid exchanges in Chinese hamster ovary cells. These in vitro studies were carried out both with
00159 | and without exogenous metabolic activation.
00160 | Fertility was unaffected when methyldopa was given to male and female rats at 100 mg/kg/day (1.7 times
00161 | the maximum daily human dose when compared on the basis of body weight; 0.2 times the maximum
00162 | daily human dose when compared on the basis of body surface area). Methyldopa decreased sperm
00163 | count, sperm motility, the number of late spermatids and the male fertility index when given to male rats
00164 | at 200 and 400 mg/kg/day. (3.3 and 6.7 times the maximum daily human dose when compared on the basis
00165 | of body weight; 0.5 and 1 times the maximum daily human dose when compared on the basis of body
00166 | surface area).
00167 | Pregnancy
00168 | Pregnancy Category B
00169 | Reproduction studies performed with methyldopa at oral doses up to 1000 mg/kg in mice, 200 mg/kg in
00170 | rabbits and 100 mg/kg in rats revealed no evidence of harm to the fetus. These doses are 16.6 times, 3.3
00171 | times and 1.7 times, respectively, the maximum daily human dose when compared on the basis of body
00172 | 
00173 | weight; 1.4 times, 1.1 times and 0.2 times, respectively, when compared on the basis of body surface
00174 | area; calculations assume a patient weight of 50 kg. There are, however, no adequate and well-
00175 | controlled studies in pregnant women in the first trimester of pregnancy. Because animal reproduction
00176 | studies are not always predictive of human response, methyldopa should be used during pregnancy only
00177 | if clearly needed.
00178 | Published reports of the use of methyldopa during all trimesters indicate that if this drug is used during
00179 | pregnancy the possibility of fetal harm appears remote. In five studies, three of which were controlled,
00180 | involving 332 pregnant hypertensive women, treatment with methyldopa was associated with an
00181 | improved fetal outcome. The majority of these women were in the third trimester when methyldopa
00182 | therapy was begun.
00183 | In one study, women who had begun methyldopa treatment between weeks 16 and 20 of pregnancy gave
00184 | birth to infants whose average head circumference was reduced by a small amount (34.2 ± 1.7 cm vs.
00185 | 34.6 ± 1.3 cm [mean ± 1 S.D.]). Long-term follow up of 195 (97.5%) of the children born to
00186 | methyldopa-treated pregnant women (including those who began treatment between weeks 16 and 20)
00187 | failed to uncover any significant adverse effect on the children. At four years of age, the developmental
00188 | delay commonly seen in children born to hypertensive mothers was less evident in those whose mothers
00189 | were treated with methyldopa during pregnancy than those whose mothers were untreated. The children
00190 | of the treated group scored consistently higher than the children of the untreated group on five major
00191 | indices of intellectual and motor development. At age seven and one-half developmental scores and
00192 | intelligence indices showed no significant differences in children of treated or untreated hypertensive
00193 | women.
00194 | Nursing Mothers
00195 | Methyldopa appears in breast milk. Therefore, caution should be exercised when methyldopa is given
00196 | to a nursing woman.
00197 | Pediatric Use
00198 | There are no well-controlled clinical trials in pediatric patients. Information on dosing in pediatric
00199 | patients is supported by evidence from published literature regarding the treatment of hypertension in
00200 | pediatric patients. (See DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION.)
00201 | Geriatric Use
00202 | Of the total number of subjects (1685) in clinical studies of methyldopa, 223 patients were 65 years of
00203 | age and over while 33 patients were 75 years of age and over. No overall differences in safety or
00204 | effectiveness were observed between these subjects and younger subjects, and other reported clinical
00205 | experience has not identified differences in responses between the elderly and younger patients, but
00206 | greater sensitivity of some older individuals cannot be ruled out. (See DOSAGE AND
00207 | ADMINISTRATION.)
00208 | This drug is known to be substantially excreted by the kidney, and the risk of toxic reactions to this drug
00209 | may be greater in patients with impaired renal function. Because elderly patients are more likely to have
00210 | decreased renal function, care should be taken in dose selection and it may be useful to monitor renal
00211 | function.
00212 | ADVERSE REACTIONS
00213 | Sedation, usually transient, may occur during the initial period of therapy or whenever the dose is
00214 | increased. Headache, asthenia, or weakness may be noted as early and transient symptoms. However,
00215 | significant adverse effects due to methyldopa have been infrequent and this agent usually is well
00216 | tolerated.
00217 | The following adverse reactions have been reported, and within each category, are listed in order of
00218 | 
00219 | decreasing severity.
00220 | Cardiovascular: Aggravation of angina pectoris, congestive heart failure, prolonged carotoid sinus
00221 | hypersensitivity, orthostatic hypotension (decrease daily dosage), edema or weight gain, bradycardia.
00222 | Digestive: Pancreatitis, colitis, vomiting, diarrhea, sialadenitis, sore or “black” tongue, nausea,
00223 | constipation, distention, flatus, dryness of mouth.
00224 | Endocrine: Hyperprolactinemia.
00225 | Hematologic: Bone marrow depression, leukopenia, granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic
00226 | anemia; positive tests for antinuclear antibody, LE cells, and rheumatoid factor, positive Coombs test.
00227 | Hepatic: Liver disorders including hepatitis, jaundice, abnormal liver function tests (see WARNINGS).
00228 | Hypersensitivity: Myocarditis, pericarditis, vasculitis, lupus-like syndrome, drug-related fever,
00229 | eosinophilia.
00230 | Nervous System/Psychiatric: Parkinsonism, Bell’s palsy, decreased mental acuity, involuntary
00231 | choreoathetotic movements, symptoms of cerebrovascular insufficiency, psychic disturbances
00232 | including nightmares and reversible mild psychoses or depression, headache, sedation, asthenia or
00233 | weakness, dizziness, lightheadedness, paresthesias.
00234 | Metabolic: Rise in BUN.
00235 | Musculoskeletal: Arthralgia, with or without joint swelling; myalgia.
00236 | Respiratory: Nasal stuffiness.
00237 | Skin: Toxic epidermal necrolysis, rash.
00238 | Urogenital: Amenorrhea, breast enlargement, gynecomastia, lactation, impotence, decreased libido.
00239 | OVERDOSAGE
00240 | Acute overdosage may produce acute hypotension with other responses attributable to brain and
00241 | gastrointestinal malfunction (excessive sedation, weakness, bradycardia, dizziness, lightheadedness,
00242 | constipation, distention, flatus, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting).
00243 | In the event of overdosage, symptomatic and supportive measures should be employed. When ingestion
00244 | is recent, gastric lavage or emesis may reduce absorption. When ingestion has been earlier, infusions
00245 | may be helpful to promote urinary excretion. Otherwise, management includes special attention to
00246 | cardiac rate and output, blood volume, electrolyte imbalance, paralytic ileus, urinary function and
00247 | cerebral activity.
00248 | Sympathomimetic drugs [e.g., levarterenol, epinephrine, metaraminol bitartrate] may be indicated.
00249 | Methyldopa is dialyzable.

00252 | DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
00253 | ADULTS
00254 | Initiation of Therapy
00255 | The usual starting dosage of methyldopa is 250 mg two or three times a day in the first 48 hours. The
00256 | daily dosage then may be increased or decreased, preferably at intervals of not less than two days, until
00257 | an adequate response is achieved. To minimize the sedation, start dosage increases in the evening. By
00258 | adjustment of dosage, morning hypotension may be prevented without sacrificing control of afternoon
00259 | blood pressure.
00260 | 50
00261 | 
00262 | When methyldopa is given to patients on other antihypertensives, the dose of these agents may need to
00263 | be adjusted to effect a smooth transition. When methyldopa is given with antihypertensives other than
00264 | thiazides, the initial dosage of methyldopa should be limited to 500 mg daily in divided doses; when
00265 | methyldopa is added to a thiazide, the dosage of thiazide need not be changed.
00266 | Maintenance Therapy
00267 | The usual daily dosage of methyldopa is 500 mg to 2 g in two to four doses. Although occasional
00268 | patients have responded to higher doses, the maximum recommended daily dosage is 3 g. Once an
00269 | effective dosage range is attained, a smooth blood pressure response occurs in most patients in 12 to 24
00270 | hours. Since methyldopa has a relatively short duration of action, withdrawal is followed by return of
00271 | hypertension usually within 48 hours. This is not complicated by an overshoot of blood pressure.
00272 | Occasionally tolerance may occur, usually between the second and third month of therapy. Adding a
00273 | diuretic or increasing the dosage of methyldopa frequently will restore effective control of blood
00274 | pressure. A thiazide may be added at any time during methyldopa therapy and is recommended if therapy
00275 | has not been started with a thiazide or if effective control of blood pressure cannot be maintained on 2 g
00276 | of methyldopa daily.
00277 | Methyldopa is largely excreted by the kidney and patients with impaired renal function may respond to
00278 | smaller doses. Syncope in older patients may be related to an increased sensitivity and advanced
00279 | arteriosclerotic vascular disease. This may be avoided by lower doses. (See PRECAUTIONS,
00280 | Geriatric Use).
00281 | PEDIATRIC PATIENTS
00282 | Initial dosage is based on 10 mg/kg of body weight daily in two to four doses. The daily dosage then is
00283 | increased or decreased until an adequate response is achieved. The maximum dosage is 65 mg/kg or 3 g
00284 | daily, whichever is less. (See PRECAUTIONS, Pediatric Use.)
